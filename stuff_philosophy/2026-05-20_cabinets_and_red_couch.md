# Stuff Philosophy — The Cabinets & The Red Couch
## May 20, 2026

**The Problem I'm Learning to Solve:**

I can separate the thing from the feeling. The object from the memory. This is the work.

---

## The Red Couch

**The Fact:** Largest piece in storage. Picked up by Clay and Adam (Teen Challenge). Gone.

**The Feeling:** Relief. Space opening up. Room to move through and process the rest.

**The Learning:** The couch was taking up physical and mental space. But it was never really *the* couch. It was what I thought it meant. Where people sat. What it represented. Now it's gone, and all of that is still here — inside me. The couch itself was just a couch.

The important part: it went somewhere useful (Teen Challenge). It's not gone to waste. It's gone to serve a new purpose.

---

## The Kitchen Cabinets

**The Moment:** When I first saw them off the wall, I felt broken. The house felt broken.

**The Insight:** But the house isn't broken. The cabinets are old, water-damaged, their time is done. And the kitchen will be better without them. New cabinets. New countertop. New sink. New faucet. It's not loss. It's transformation.

**The Work:** Separating:
- The object (old cabinet, water-stained wood, wonky hinges)
- From the feeling (the house is broken, I am failing, things are falling apart)
- From the new reality (the kitchen will be beautiful, the house will be restored, this is good)

**The Practice:** Looking at something old and worn and saying: "You had your time. Now it's time for something new. And that's okay."

---

## The Pattern I'm Noticing

Every time I let something go:
1. First: attachment, resistance, feeling
2. Then: looking closely at the actual thing (old, worn, not what I thought it was)
3. Then: relief, space, clarity
4. Then: the memory stays, the thing is gone, and both are fine

The Pyrex I'm keeping because it still speaks to me. The bowl I'm selling because it has a new kind of value. The red couch I let go because it was taking up space I needed. The old cabinets being replaced because a better version is coming.

It's not about emptiness. It's about intention.

---

## The Question

What if the real skill isn't learning to hold on? What if it's learning to know *what* to hold and *when* to let go?

The cabinets had their time. The red couch had its purpose. Mom's memory doesn't live in her Pyrex — it lives in me. And that's enough. That's actually everything.
