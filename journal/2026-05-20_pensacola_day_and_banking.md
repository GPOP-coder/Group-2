Got what I needed:

Small financial decisions that add up. That's stewardship.

**Afternoon (4:41 PM) — The Cabinets and the Red Couch**

Clay and Adam from Teen Challenge came by. They're movers — that's what they do. Picked up the old kitchen cabinets from the house. They weren't thrilled about it at first, but they came around. That's how it works sometimes. You find people who can help, and you make it work.

Before we got started, we swung by the storage space. They picked up the red couch. The biggest piece in there. It's been taking up space, holding space that could be used for other things. Now it's gone. That frees everything up.

**The Broken Feeling:**

When I first saw the cabinets off the wall, something shifted inside me. I felt a little broken. The house felt broken. Like everything that was attached to the house was broken too.

But I'm getting better at this. Separating things from feelings. Separating objects from memories. That's probably good. I was here to witness it, to process it. And that matters.

Looking at the cabinets now, really looking at them — they're old. Water damaged. Hinges wonky. They've had their time. The kitchen will be better without them. New cabinets. New countertop. New sink. New faucet I picked up last night. It's good. It's actually good.

**Reflection:**

This day was a mix of the physical work (faucets, hazardous waste, cabinets) and the invisible work (banking, POA, account management). Both matter. Both are part of taking care of the property and taking care of Dad.
