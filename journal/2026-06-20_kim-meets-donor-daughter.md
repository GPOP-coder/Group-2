## Kim's Write-Up (verbatim, submitted to Taylor Wilson, NMed, June 26, 2026)

> Heart Transplant Recipient Meets Donor's Daughter in Emotional First Encounter
>
> June 20, 2026 | Omaha, Nebraska
>
> An emotional and deeply moving meeting took place Saturday, June 20, 2026, in Denver, CO at the National Transplant Games. A Nebraska Medicine heart transplant recipient, Kim Castellano, met Thomaree Holland, the 15-year-old daughter of her donor, Kristian Nicole Holland, for the first time. Kim's daughter and one of her dedicated caregivers throughout the transplant journey escorted her to Denver. Virginia, a family friend, attended with Thomaree. The meeting, facilitated by XVIVO, brought together two individuals connected by a life-saving transplant performed on October 31, 2024, at Nebraska Medicine.
>
> **Private Meeting Marks Powerful First Connection**
>
> The day began with a private meeting between Kim and Thomaree in a quiet room, allowing both to share personal stories and reflect on the profound connection they now share. Kim presented Thomaree with a special necklace to commemorate their new bond. Castellano, who received Kristian Holland's heart nearly two years ago, spoke about her transplant journey, including the uncertainty leading up to the surgery and the renewed sense of life she has experienced since. Thomaree shared memories of her mother, describing her personality, strength, and the lasting impact she continues to have on her family. The intimate conversation created space for both grief and gratitude, highlighting the human side of organ donation—a process often discussed in clinical terms but experienced in deeply personal ways.
>
> **Sharing Their Story Publicly**
>
> Following their private meeting, Kim and Thomaree participated in media interviews, offering a broader audience a glimpse into their experience. Castellano emphasized the life-saving impact of organ donation, expressing ongoing gratitude to both XVIVO and Nebraska Medicine for the gift she received on October 31, 2024. She also spoke about her commitment to honoring her donor through the way she lives each day. Thomaree, demonstrating composure beyond her years, spoke about her mother not only as a donor but as a person—a parent whose legacy continues through both memory and the life she helped save.
>
> Together, their story illustrated the intersection of loss and hope inherent in organ donation, underscoring the importance of donor registration and awareness.
>
> **Community and Connection at XVIVO Event**
>
> The day concluded with a Donor/Recipient VIP event where Kim and Thomaree joined medical professionals, transplant advocates, and staff from the company's Denver team. The gathering provided an opportunity to connect with others affected by organ donation and transplantation, including both recipients and donor families. Attendees shared experiences, recognized medical advancements, and honored the generosity that makes transplantation possible. For both, the event served as a reminder that their story is part of a larger community—one built on compassion, innovation, and life-saving care.
>
> **A Lasting Bond**
>
> While the meeting marked their first encounter, both Kim and Thomaree now share an enduring connection rooted in Kristian Nicole Holland's legacy. Their interaction offered a powerful example of how organ donation can unite lives in unexpected and meaningful ways—turning tragedy into hope and ensuring that one life continues to affect others in profound ways.

**Name spelling note:** CNN spells the donor's daughter's name as "Tamari Holland" and the donor as "Christian Nicole Holland." Our records use "Thomaree" and "Kristian" — the CNN spellings are likely phonetic. Confirm correct spelling with family.

### New Details from CNN Not in Kim's Write-Up

**Kim's heart has a name: Boo.**
Named for Halloween — the day of the transplant (October 31, 2024). At her first heartversary, Kim set a place at the table for her donor before she knew who it was. *"My donor was with me."*

**The Boo coincidence.**
When Thomaree (Tamari) learned the heart was named Boo, she was moved: *"I always say Boo, and my mom, like, she would say Boo. So it was just, like, such a coincidence that you gave it that name, where we say that a lot. That was just kind of special to me."*

**Tamari had been saving Kim's letters.**
After Kim sent a thank-you letter to the donor family, they wrote back. Letters turned into texts. Tamari kept every one.

**Gifts were exchanged both ways.**
Kim gave the heart-shaped necklace. Tamari gave Kim **a bookmark covered with her mother's photos** — a detail not in Kim's own write-up.

**The organ preservation technology angle.**
CNN noted Kim struggled to find a match and that a new organ preservation technology (XVIVO) allowed her to receive a heart from farther away than previously possible. This is why XVIVO facilitated the meeting.

**What they both came home saying.**
*"I can't speak for her, but I know we both coming home said, that just felt right. Like, that just felt like something we needed."*
Tamari: *"Knowing that her heart is really in a better place, like, knowing it, seeing it, it definitely makes it easier to go on about my day."*

**Future plans:** They intend to meet again and potentially compete together in future Transplant Games.
