# May 26, 2026 — Storage, Letting Go, and Old Mustang Batteries

**Morning, 10:30 AM — ExtraSpace Storage**

Went through five boxes today that were packed and moved just yesterday. There's still this tension inside me about certain things — Mom's Pyrex, that old IHOP mug, the Waffle House mug. I keep reaching for them and then setting them back down. Not yet. Not today.

Headed over to the Redemption Center with a couple boxes to donate. Better off in someone else's hands who will actually use them, enjoy them. That's what matters now.

Met Carol Johnson there — old family friend. Good to see her. Made the donation feel less like loss and more like passing forward.

About two boxes total came out of storage today. Each one a small step in the direction I'm trying to go. Not giving away everything that mattered to Mom or that matters to me. Just the stuff that's waiting for someone else to love it.

I did keep a few pieces. The Pyrex — some of it I'm holding onto because it still speaks to me. And there's one bowl in particular that I think could do well on eBay. Maybe $250 if I can get the right listing. That one feels like it has a different kind of value now — not sentimental, but worth passing on to someone who collects these things.

---

**Evening, Approaching 6:00 PM — Heading Home**

Getting ready to head over to the house. Need to grab that old Mustang battery and take it to Walmart. And I want to see the property — how it's looking, if anyone's been there working on it. Check the progress. The renovation feels real now when you can see it with your own eyes.

Picked up two faucet choices on the way — one from Home Depot, one from Lowe's. Kitchen decisions. Small choices that feel big when they're about a house, about a home.

---

**Reflections:**

The struggle with stuff is real. Mom's dishes. The mugs. The things that hold memories. But I'm learning that holding onto the *object* doesn't hold onto the memory. The memory is already inside me. The mug is just a mug. The Pyrex is just Pyrex. Beautiful, yes. But it doesn't need to be in my storage unit gathering dust.

The ones I'm keeping — the bowl, some of the Pyrex — those are the ones that either still speak to me directly or have a new kind of purpose. Everything else gets to find a new life elsewhere.

Carol Johnson reminded me of what it means to have friends who know your history. Those moments matter.

And the Mustang battery? The renovation progress? Those are the practical things that ground the bigger work — the work of building, maintaining, moving forward. One battery at a time. One decision at a time.
