# The Exchange — Hal Daub Interview
*June 19, 2026 — recorded from voice memo of June 11, 2026 interview*

---

## The Moment

Near the end of the Hal Daub interview for the BTTR Bob Kerrey Pedestrian Bridge coffee table book, conducted at Daub's home on June 11, 2026.

Present: Hal Daub, Pete Castellano (BTTR President / book producer), Jim and John (Legacy videography team), Larry Foster (Director of Parks & Recreation under Daub).

---

## Verbatim Exchange (corrected from recording)

**Pete:** "I'm the only person who can say I've worked for both of you."

**Daub:** "You did, you worked for both of us. Boy, did I get shit for hiring you."

**Pete:** "I bet."

**Daub:** "Boy did I get crap..."

**Pete:** "But I got it too."

**Daub:** "For going to work for me?"

**Pete:** "But I was happy to do it. I thought it was a smart move on my part."

**Daub:** "It was a smart move on my part. Your wife thought so too. She thought it was OK."

**Pete:** "She gave up a job going to Seattle."

**Daub:** "I know, I know."

**Pete:** "Washington. She was like, 'We're going to stay right here 'cause you are working for the Mayor.'"

---

## Context

**The political moment:**
Kerrey's Senate term ended. Incoming Senator Ben Nelson — former Nebraska governor — chose to bring in most of his state staff and only one or two Kerrey people. Kerrey himself supported Nelson, and Kerrey's staff (Pete included) had effectively worked a second full-time job on Nelson's campaign. When Nelson won, Pete was job-hunting.

That is when Daub made the offer.

Daub hired Pete knowing exactly what it looked like: a Republican mayor bringing someone straight out of a Democratic senator's office, at the same time the Kerrey network was organizing the mayoral campaign that would ultimately oust Daub. Pete served as Daub's point person on the streetcar / light rail project for the months leading up to the election.

**The cost:**
When Mayor Mike Fahey took office, many of the Kerrey staffers and campaign workers — people who had been Pete's long-time friends — saw him as a turncoat. They pinned their dislike of Daub on Pete. The consequence: instead of transitioning into Fahey's administration as the riverfront guy (the natural next move), Pete was frozen out.

**The pivot:**
Pete turned his attention to founding **Back to the River, Inc. (BTTR)** as a nonprofit — channeling the riverfront work into a civic organization instead of a government role. That pivot eventually led to a job at Ameristar, which became the start of a career in Workforce Management.

**Kim's role:**
Kim had been offered the position of elementary school principal at **Oak Harbor Public Schools** — the largest district on Whidbey Island, WA, near the naval air station, north of Seattle across Puget Sound. She declined to stay in Omaha because Pete was working for Mayor Daub. Daub remembered this detail unprompted, more than thirty years later.

*"This time was so formative."* — Pete Castellano, June 19, 2026
