# Stuff Philosophy
## Pete Castellano

> **This is really not about stuff.**

---

## The Core

Objects, value, togetherness, and letting go.

The stuff philosophy grew out of a simple observation: the things we own carry meaning that has nothing to do with their price, and a price that has nothing to do with their meaning. Most of us never untangle those two threads. This is an attempt to.

---

## Price Is Not Value

A Brother laser printer sells at auction for $33.90. It works. It has Genuine toner installed. It's worth $150 used on a fair day, $250 new. It sells for $33.90.

Why?

The seller isn't wrong. They're not confused about the market. They're optimizing for something other than the item's value — space, time, urgency, or the simple fact that they already have a replacement and this machine has served its purpose. Waiting would cost them more than the gap between $33.90 and $150. So they price for their situation, not the item's worth.

The buyer who knows the value and has the patience to watch for exactly this moment captures that gap. Not by exploiting anyone — the seller got what they needed. The buyer got what they waited for. The transaction is fair on both sides, even though the price looks wrong on the surface.

**The lesson:** Not all prices are tied to the value of the item. Knowing the value and having the patience to wait for the right situation is itself a skill, and a form of value creation.

But this isn't really about printers. It never was.

---

## What It's Really About

The same dynamic plays out everywhere:

- A relationship that ends not because it lost its value but because the timing, the circumstances, the cost of staying exceeded the cost of letting go.
- A house that sells below market because the seller needs it gone more than they need the extra $20,000.
- A piece of advice, freely given, that lands at exactly the moment someone is ready to hear it — and would have meant nothing a year earlier.
- A memory that was never in the plate. Or the couch. Or the sock. The object was just where we stored it for a while.

**Value is real. Price is situational. The two only occasionally align.**
