# AI Prompts Toolkit

Reusable self-coaching/reflection prompt templates — copy one, fill in the brackets, paste to Claude. Source: YouTube, "5 AI Prompts To Fix Your Entire Life In 1 Day" (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FPnFp8vFM9k), added to this repo 2026-09-02.

Ties to the broader "AI as journal" goal in CLAUDE.md (Voice Memos → transcript → Claude → organized reflection) — these are structured entry points for that same kind of work, for specific situations rather than open-ended journaling.

---

## 1. Conflict Rehearsal

Use when preparing for a hard conversation with someone specific.

```
Act as the person I am in conflict with or the person I'm trying to communicate with. Do not pretend that you know their inner life. Here's the situation in my own words: [...]
Here's what I plan to say: [...]
Do not take my side. Argue their case as they might, assuming they are smart and fair and have reasons that make sense to them. Then tell me the following:
1. What they most likely feel that I'm not seeing.
2. The weakest part of my argument.
3. What they may need to hear from me.
Do not flatter me. Do not turn this into a therapy session. Help me rehearse the actual conversation.
```

## 2. Transformation Map

Use when moving into a new chapter/role and need a practical bridge, not inspiration.

```
Act as my transformation partner. You are my map maker. Map out my next move. Here's where I am right now: [...]
Here's where I'm trying to go: [...]
Interview me, one question at a time, to find the gap. Then give me an action plan and a map that bridges that gap. After the interview, respond to these five questions:
1. Which of my strengths still will carry over?
2. Which strengths will become liabilities in this new chapter?
3. What I need to leave behind, even though it's worked before.
4. What I have never built but is now needed.
5. What I should test for at least 30 days before I make a bigger commitment.
Do not give me abstract purpose advice. Do not flatter me. Do not give me a ten year life plan. Give me a practical transformation map, and do not suck up to me.
```

## 3. Decision Simulator

Use when facing a real fork with two live options.

```
Act as my decision simulator. I am facing a fork ahead.
Option A is [...]
Option B is [...]
Here's what I know: [...]
Interview me one question at a time to understand the decision. And then run a separate pre-mortem on each option. For each one, imagine that it's two years later, and that choice failed badly, and work backward from there, and tell me why. Treat them independently so I can compare how each road fails differently. And then for each road, tell me these three things:
1. The most likely way it will fail.
2. The warning signs I should see first.
3. The cost I cannot reverse.
Do not tell me what to choose. Be very rigorous and systematic about your analysis.
```

## 4. Debrief Partner

Use after something difficult has already happened — processing, not deciding.

```
Act as a debrief partner for something difficult that I have gone through.
Here's what happened: [...]
The story I've been telling myself about this is this: [...]
Interview me one question at a time. Do not comfort me or try to find a solution. I just want to talk. And then help me understand the following four things:
1. The facts of what happened.
2. The meaning I keep attributing that isn't about the facts.
3. What was mine to control and what wasn't?
4. One thing to carry forward. One thing to leave behind.
```

## 5. Behavior Investigator

Use to examine a repeating personal pattern — customize the pattern description each time.

```
Act as an investigator for my behavior. This is the pattern I keep repeating: [...]
I want to understand three things:
1. Where my blind spots may be.
2. Why I keep avoiding them.
3. How do I catch them before they're triggered?
Interview me one question at a time. Unpack my answer and based on it, ask me the next better question. Use only my answer as evidence. When you're ready, give me your best hypotheses, the evidence behind it and the evidence that's still missing. Be respectful, candid, and direct. Do not motivate me or flatter me. Do not suck up to me.
```

---

## Common thread across all five

Every prompt explicitly forbids flattery/comfort/motivational framing and insists on an interview-first structure (one question at a time) before any analysis. Worth preserving that shape if writing new ones for this toolkit — the value is in being pushed, not soothed.
