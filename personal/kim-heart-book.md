# Kim's Heart Book — Notes & Source Material

Kim has been reluctant to write her story. Pete believes it's extraordinary enough to warrant a book. The CNN national coverage (June 27, 2026) — the day after Kim submitted her own write-up to Nebraska Medicine — may be the catalyst.

---

## The Story in Brief

- **Born with a congenital heart defect.** Kim was not expected to live past childhood — let alone reach college.
- **February 1967:** Kim became the **first child in Nebraska to have a pacemaker implanted** — procedure performed at Nebraska Medical Center. She was 2 years old.
- She carried that pacemaker for nearly 58 years — from age 2 until her transplant in 2024.
- **October 31, 2024:** Kim receives heart transplant at Nebraska Medicine. Heart #587. Donor: Kristian Nicole Holland, mother of five, Wichita KS.
- Kim named her heart **Boo** — for Halloween. Unknowingly, Kristian's family also used "Boo" as a term of endearment.
- Kim sent a thank-you letter to the donor family. They wrote back. Letters became texts.
- Kim sent a heartbeat bear (heartbeat recorded from her echo) to Tamara Holland, Kristian's mother.
- **June 20, 2026:** Kim and her daughter (Jennie) met Thomaree Holland, Kristian's 15-year-old daughter, at the National Transplant Games in Denver — facilitated by XVIVO.
- Gifts exchanged: Kim gave a heart-shaped necklace; Thomaree gave a bookmark covered with her mother's photos.
- Kim held back tears through the private meeting and media interviews — broke when her daughter hugged Thomaree.

## Notes for the Book

- Kim is writing in her own voice already — her Nebraska Medicine write-up is articulate and moving. She doesn't know she's already started.
- **The arc is 60 years long.** First child in Nebraska with a pacemaker in 1967. Heart transplant at the same institution in 2024. Nebraska Medicine is woven through her entire life.
- The Boo coincidence is the opening paragraph.
- The image of the two daughters hugging is the emotional center.
- The XVIVO organ preservation technology angle gives it a science/hope dimension beyond the personal story.
- One donor, five children left behind — and a recipient who named the heart after the day it arrived.
- **Kim's own words from the article:** *"It was a moment I had imagined many times. To finally meet someone who knew Kristian, loved her, and carries her memory every day was incredibly meaningful."*
