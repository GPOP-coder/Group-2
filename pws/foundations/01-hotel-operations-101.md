## 2. Hotel Types

### Limited (Select) Service
- Fewer amenities
- Surface parking only
- No club lounge
- Reduced or no bell service
- Limited or no Food & Beverage
- Less staff
- Traditionally suburban

### Full Service
- Room service
- Executive lounge
- Laundry service
- Multiple restaurants
- Bar or lounge
- Meeting space
- Urban, suburban, airport locations

## 3. Branded vs. Non-Branded

### Branded
- Common reservation system
- National marketing and sales
- Brand standards and quality controls
- TA commission processing
- Annual inspections
- Worldwide recognition
- Marketing and reservation fees

### Non-Branded (Independent)
- More unique product
- Self-reliant for reservations, marketing, standards, training
- No fees, no access to mass reservation systems

## 5. Management Types

### Brand Managed

## 7. Property Management Systems (PMS)
*The operational backbone of a hotel.*

**Core functions:**
- Room inventory management, reservations, and guestroom assignments
- Guest check-in, check-out, billing, and accounting
- Operational and marketing reports
- Interfaces with other hotel systems
- Configurable for single-property or multi-property operations

**Common vendors:**
- Oracle Opera (formerly Micros Fidelio) — cloud-based, favored by large brands
- On-Q (Hilton proprietary)
- Galaxy/LightSpeed (Starwood proprietary)
- Maestro — leading vendor for independent hotels
- WebRezPro, Frontdesk Anywhere — web-based, small to medium properties

## 10. Guest Satisfaction
*Source: Cornell Hospitality Industry Perspectives, July 2010*

### Why It Matters Financially
- Costs 3–5x more to attract a new guest than retain an existing one
- Previous guests strongly influence others through word of mouth
- Higher guest satisfaction → higher loyalty → better financial performance (higher occupancy, ADR, RevPAR)

### What Drives Satisfaction
- How the service was delivered matters more than the outcome
- Dissatisfaction occurs when perception does not meet expectation
- Value perception fluctuates — guests may equate value with low price

### Spending Behavior
- Satisfied guests spend more on subsequent stays
- Loyalty translates directly to revenue per available room

## 13. Rooms Division

### Front Office Roles

**Front Office Supervisor**
- Front desk oversight
- Fixed tasks, break coverage, peak period support, service recovery

**Guest Service Agent**
- Check-in: establish credit, room assignment, customer questions
- Check-out: present folio, zero balance, final guest contact
- Fixed tasks: room assignments, group preparation, bucket checks, due-outs

**Reservations**
- Book individual rooms and group blocks
- Room lists, guaranteed no-show processing

**Switchboard (PBX)**
- Answer incoming and internal calls
- Conduit to Engineering for maintenance issues

---

### Night Audit — General Duties
- Manager on Duty / Security function
- Front desk responsibility (check-ins, etc.)
- Verification and correction of the day's work:
  - Guest ledger: rates match market segment codes

## 14. Room Type Framework
*Pete Castellano / PWS — developed from peer conversation May 19, 2026*

### The Core Concept
There is no single cookie-cutter room cleaning standard. But there are dials — parameters that, when set correctly, produce a defensible, observable standard.

### Checkout Time Benchmarks
*(Illustrative — validated by observation)*

| Room Type | Checkout Time |
|---|---|
| Basic select-service room | ~20 minutes |
| Standard full-service room | ~30 minutes |
| Enhanced amenities (iron, coffee station, etc.) | ~30–40 minutes |
| Suite with sitting area, multiple fixtures | ~45–60 minutes |
| Resort room with wardrobe room, vanity, balcony | ~60–90 minutes |

### Parameters That Drive Room Time
- Square footage
- Bed count (1 vs. 2, king vs. doubles)
- Bathroom configuration (tub vs. shower, glass door vs. curtain)
- Balcony (clean or not, sliding door)
- Coffee station (basic vs. elaborate)
- Closet type (rack behind door vs. full wardrobe room)
- Amenity format (bottles vs. pump dispensers)
- Extra furniture (lamps, chairs, tables, decorative items)
- Laundry handling (room vs. drop point)
- Specialty items (ski equipment, golf clubs, umbrellas)
- Pool/beach adjacency (sand, towels, extra cleaning)

### Stayover vs. Checkout
Stayover and checkout are not the same standard. The parameters differ:
- Checkout: full reset, all surfaces, full linen change
- Stayover: partial reset based on guest presence and property policy
- A 30-minute checkout room may have a 20-minute stayover — or longer if it's a beach/party property

### The Critical Distinction
**Hours per occupied room is not a labor standard for housekeeping.**
It is a measurement — useful for benchmarking, not for scheduling.

The correct standard is built on **checkouts vs. stayovers**, because:
- You do not clean occupied rooms
- Average length of stay changes throughout the year
- A shift-heavy checkout week vs. a stayover-dominant week produces legitimately different labor needs under the same HPOR target
- Executive housekeepers should not be criticized for being "off standard" when the mix has shifted — unless you've accounted for mix
