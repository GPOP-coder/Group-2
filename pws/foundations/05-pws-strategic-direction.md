## 1. The Consulting Gap — Core Thesis

**Unifocus devalued consulting → PWS is the consulting practice Unifocus used to be → clients prove it with their wallets.**

### What Happened at Unifocus
Post-Riverside acquisition, Unifocus repositioned as a software company. Implementations became checklist-driven. Pre-implementation analysis — the work that set up interfaces, understood the client's operation, and ensured the system was configured correctly before go-live — was largely abandoned.

The May 2026 monthly consultant meeting made this explicit: a veteran consultant asked "what changed?" regarding interface automation and pre-implementation analysis. The room had no good answer.

The internal consultant role has become: checklist management, folder structures, billing categories, status letters. That is process administration, not consulting.

### What PWS Does Instead
Pete brings the full consulting and analysis skillset to every engagement:
- Diagnosis and observation
- Labor standards methodology
- Interface architecture and planning
- Data validation and go-live readiness
- Post-implementation recovery

This is what Unifocus consultants used to do. It is the gap PWS fills.

### Client Proof
- **MHBR:** $101K/year in identified savings. 6.7x ROI on Pete's fee. Genevieve called because housekeeping productivity slipped — not because the software broke.
- **Mohonk:** Two previous Unifocus consultants failed. Pete solved it through observation, patience, and relationship. Ralph called it "working a miracle."
- **Baha Mar:** Inherited an incomplete implementation mess. Forensic analysis and judgment, not button-clicking.

### The Support-to-Consulting Handoff — and Why It Won't Scale Down (added 2026-07-31)

**How support actually operates, observed via UNIFOCUS-235850 (Rosewood Miramar BQT, 7/28/26):** Unifocus's support department's real boundary of ownership is "is the connection/file working" — not "is the data mapped correctly." Once a file is technically flowing (or once it once flowed), support treats the ticket as resolved and permanently hands mapping accuracy and all downstream errors to consulting/implementation. There is no clean handback — consulting inherits the file's correctness forever, with no defined end state where support re-owns it. Rosewood Miramar's BQT file was still zeroing out after a year; support's answer was "it's mapped to the old interface" plus a sample file, then marked the ticket resolved — Pete's actual diagnostic questions (is the automation broken, is a file coming, what's needed to revive it) went unanswered.

**Why this matters strategically:** John Lockyer told Pete directly (7/30/26 call) that Unifocus's plan for winning the select-service market (Hampton/Courtyard-tier properties) is a simpler, more intuitive platform that needs **little or no implementation team** — CSMs get properties over the hump, not consultants. But the support-to-consulting handoff above shows where the *real* ongoing cost of "getting the data right" actually lives: it doesn't disappear once the software is simpler, it just has nowhere to land if there's no implementation team left to absorb it. Someone still has to own file/interface accuracy after go-live — that work either:
1. Gets pushed onto **Integration** itself (the connection/automation layer has to become self-validating and much smarter than it is today — not just "is data flowing" but "is it flowing *correctly*"), or
2. Keeps quietly costing Unifocus consulting hours anyway, undermining the "lean, no-implementation-team" pitch for select-service.

**Pete's read:** this is a real tension in Unifocus's stated strategy, not a solved problem — and it's exactly the kind of gap PWS's consulting/architectural work fills today (see rate-justification evidence in `pws/foundations/07-unifocus-contract-rewrite-2026.md`). Worth having in back pocket for the eventual role/rate conversation with John, since it's first-party evidence (not just Pete's opinion) that "shrink implementation, lean on software" has an unsolved cost hiding in exactly this handoff.

## 2. PWS Positioning

### The Wraparound
PWS is not a software company. PWS is the management practice, the labor standards methodology, the training, and the consulting that wraps around the software.

The software (Unifocus, HotSOS, OnTrack, or other) is the operating system. PWS is what makes it work.

> "I'm developing the Platinum Workforce Strategies wraparound. The management practice, the labor standards, the training — the religion, almost. Thou shalt do this. And then the why."
> — Pete Castellano

### Powered by Unifocus
Pete holds a separate referral/lead contract with Unifocus. This enables a "Powered by Unifocus" positioning:
- Pete identifies the client need and scopes the engagement
- Pete handles consulting, standards development, and implementation
- Unifocus provides software licensing and infrastructure support
- Pete bills for consulting; Unifocus bills for software

This model allows Pete to be the face, the expert, and the relationship — without building or maintaining software.

### The Why Matters
Most implementations tell managers what to do. PWS tells them why.

When people understand the why, they step forward together. They trust the standard. They defend it upward. They use the system proactively.

## 3. Pete's Competitive Advantages

### Observation Methodology
Pete built an app to record and time direct observations of room attendants and other hotel staff. This is not the old Darius-style time-and-motion study. It is systematic, relationship-based observation:
- Build rapport first (half a day minimum before guards drop)
- Record what actually happens: cart trips, supply runs, what they bring, what they take
- Notice what management doesn't see: stained laundry going back in, supplies not deployed, half a par sitting in hallway boxes
- Turn observation into defensible standards

> "I can watch individuals work, or I can watch everybody work for a year. That's what analysis is, really. And that's where the truth is."
> — Pete Castellano

### Percentile Analysis
Pete's proprietary method for calibrating labor standards using historical volume data broken into percentile buckets. Converts "dead" and "slammed" into usable numbers. Defends standards against manager pushback with data.

### AI-Assisted Configuration Audit
Pete developed a methodology for using AI (Claude) to audit Unifocus KBI configuration reports comparatively across multiple properties. Applied at Baha Mar across 4 properties — identified gaps, wrong formulas, and inconsistent configurations in under a minute. *(See Document 3 for full methodology.)*

### The Gutter System
Pete's framework for setting labor standards as a bounded range rather than a single number. Three gutters: minimum to be open, normal operating range, stretch/peak. The standard lives in the lane. *(See Document 2 / `02-labor-management-foundations.md` for full methodology.)*

### Road Presence
Pete wants to be on the road. Loves travel. Willing to go onsite for observation, relationship-building, and validation that cannot be done remotely. This is a differentiator — most retired or semi-retired labor management experts want to work remotely.
