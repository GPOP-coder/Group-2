## Implementation Philosophy

### Labor Standards ≠ Auto-Scheduling
- **Timeline reality:** Can't do labor standards + implementation in 3 weeks
- **Proper approach:** 
  1. Standards build (weeks 1-4)
  2. Wait for business cycle rotation (weeks 4-12)
  3. *Then* discuss auto-scheduling (weeks 12+)
- **Unifocus mistake:** Tries to do everything at once; implementation fails or feels rushed

### Target User (Not Department Manager)
- **Director of Finance:** "Do my actual vs. standard align with my projected hours?" (That's all they care about)
- **Not:** "Who gets scheduled Friday?" (department manager problem; solve locally, not software)
- **Akram's rule:** "If you're Director of Finance involved in who's scheduled, you're doing it wrong"

### Real-World Staffing Dynamics
**Cashiers (cage, balancing $2M drawer):** Reliable, follow schedule exactly  
**Cocktail servers (end of month, post-rent):** Unreliable, need 150% overscheduling just to hit actual vs. standard  
**Cross-training trend (post-COVID):** Room attendants filling public area roles; secondary job codes not supported by legacy time/attendance systems; cultural resistance from clients

**Akram's takeaway:** "Hit actual vs. standard; I don't care *how* you got there"
