## What Each Quadrant Means

### TOP LEFT: PROJECTED
**System**: "Based on your forecast, here's what you need"

- **Input:** Forecasted volumes (guest demand)
- **Process:** Forecasted volumes × labor standard = hours needed
- **Output:** "You should schedule X hours"
- **Logic:** Standard is fixed; volume is variable → hours flex with demand
- **Use:** Planning baseline; what SHOULD be on the schedule

### TOP RIGHT: SCHEDULED
**Manager**: "Here's what I actually put on the schedule"

- **Input:** Manager's decisions (who, when, how many)
- **Output:** Actual hours scheduled
- **Logic:** Manager's plan may match forecast or diverge
- **Use:** Reality check; what manager actually committed to

### PLANNING COMPARISON (Top Row)
**Question:** "Did manager schedule to forecast?"  
**Meaning:** Did the manager create a schedule that aligns with forecasted volume?  
**If aligned:** Good planning → likely to hit standard  
**If misaligned:** Schedule won't match volume → risk of over/understaffing

---

### BOTTOM LEFT: STANDARD
**System**: "Based on actual volume, here's what you should have done"

- **Input:** Actual volumes that occurred (what really happened)
- **Process:** Actual volumes × labor standard = expected hours
- **Output:** "You should have produced/delivered X"
- **Logic:** Standard is fixed; actual volume is the reality → this becomes your BUDGET
- **Use:** Measurement baseline; the goal for execution

### BOTTOM RIGHT: ACTUAL
**Manager/Team**: "Here's what we actually accomplished"

- **Input:** Actual labor output, productivity, service delivery
- **Output:** Hours actually worked, tasks completed, guests served
- **Logic:** What really happened on the floor
- **Use:** Reality check; what team actually did vs. what they should do

### EXECUTION COMPARISON (Bottom Row)
**Question:** "Did team execute to standard?"  
**Meaning:** Did the team perform as efficiently as the agreed-upon standard?  
**If aligned:** Actual = Standard → on budget → predictable labor costs  
**If misaligned:** Actual ≠ Standard → off budget → opportunity/problem to address

## The Logic Flow

```
VOLUME (Guest Demand)
    ↓
    ├─ FORECAST Volume → × Standard → PROJECTED hours needed
    │                                      ↓
    │                                  Manager sees this
    │                                      ↓
    │                              SCHEDULED (manager's plan)
    │
    ├─ ACTUAL Volume → × Standard → STANDARD hours (budget)
                                         ↓
                                    Team works the schedule
                                         ↓
                                    ACTUAL (what happened)

COMPARISON: Did we plan right? (Projected ↔ Scheduled)
COMPARISON: Did we execute right? (Standard ↔ Actual)
```

## Key Insights

### 1. Standard = Flexible Budget
- Standard is **NOT fixed hours** — it's a **ratio** (hours per unit of volume)
- When volume goes up, standard hours go up proportionally
- When volume goes down, standard hours go down proportionally
- **If actual = standard, you're on budget** (regardless of absolute hours)

### 2. Guests Run Your Business
- Guest demand (volume) drives everything
- Volume drives the standard → standard is your budget
- No mystery: "We were busy" becomes "We were 115% to standard"
- Measurement replaces opinion

### 3. Unifocus Is a Comparison Machine
**The Secret Sauce: "Compared to what?"**

Instead of vague statements:
- ❌ "We were busy"
- ❌ "We were understaffed"  
- ❌ "People worked hard"

Use measurement:
- ✅ "We were 120% to standard (high volume, executed well)"
- ✅ "We were 85% to standard (low volume, overstaffed)"
- ✅ "We achieved 98% to standard (executed efficiently)"

### 4. Two Different Conversations

**For CFO / Director of Finance:**
- "Are actual hours aligned with standard?"
- "Is my labor cost in line with volume?"
- "Am I on budget?"
- *Cares about: Actual vs. Standard (bottom row)*

**For Manager / Operations:**
- "Did I schedule right for the forecast?"
- "Is my team executing to standard?"
- "Where are we deviating?"
- *Cares about: Both rows (planning and execution)*

## Operational Examples

### Scenario 1: Busy Night, Good Execution
- Forecasted: 500 guests
- Scheduled: 45 hours (based on forecast)
- Actual guests: 520
- Standard hours (520 guests × standard): 47 hours
- Actual hours worked: 46 hours
- **Analysis:** 
  - Projected vs. Scheduled: ✓ Manager planned well
  - Standard vs. Actual: ✓ Team executed at 98% to standard
  - **Result:** Busy night, but controlled; on budget

### Scenario 2: Slow Night, Over-scheduled
- Forecasted: 200 guests
- Scheduled: 28 hours
- Actual guests: 180
- Standard hours (180 guests × standard): 22 hours
- Actual hours worked: 26 hours
- **Analysis:**
  - Projected vs. Scheduled: ✗ Manager over-scheduled for forecast
  - Standard vs. Actual: ✗ Team executed at 118% to standard
  - **Result:** Slow night; over-staffed; above budget

### Scenario 3: Good Forecast, Poor Execution
- Forecasted: 400 guests
- Scheduled: 40 hours (based on forecast)
- Actual guests: 395
- Standard hours (395 guests × standard): 39.5 hours
- Actual hours worked: 45 hours
- **Analysis:**
  - Projected vs. Scheduled: ✓ Manager planned well
  - Standard vs. Actual: ✗ Team executed at 114% to standard
  - **Result:** Good planning; poor execution; over budget; coaching opportunity

## Questions This Infographic Answers

1. **"Are we on budget?"** → Compare Standard ↔ Actual
2. **"Did we plan right?"** → Compare Projected ↔ Scheduled
3. **"What should we expect with this volume?"** → Look at Standard
4. **"Did the manager do their job?"** → Compare Projected ↔ Scheduled
5. **"Did the team execute?"** → Compare Standard ↔ Actual
6. **"Why were we off?"** → Look at which quadrants diverged
