## Meeting Summary (as generated by Read.ai/Teams)

The meeting reviewed UniFocus implementation progress, consultant roles, on-site scope, integration status, training expectations, and reporting capabilities for HM Alpha properties. The team confirmed five hotels completed in wave one and the next five properties are scheduled to start implementation.

Implementation timeline and logistics were defined: typical configuration is a one-week on-site consultant visit to build and test the system against the property labor budget, followed by a gap week for reconciliation and then one week of on-site training before going live. Consultants will build forecasts, meal periods, variables tied to hotel metrics, and labor standards live during configuration; training is delivered during the second visit and scheduled follow-ups are provided during post-go-live support. Manager training is approximately 2 to 2.5 hours; admin/property champion training is extended and requires attendees to use laptops and the live system.

Technical integrations and data sources were summarized: core payroll/timekeeping imports come from Paychex, most volume/transaction data comes from ProfitSword, and banquet granularity may require additional sources (Envision, Delphi, CI). Interfaces are prebuilt and require property-level mapping. Contract labor imports use WorkRecords. Approved PTO must be recorded in Paychex to appear in UniFocus.

Consultant scope and hotel responsibilities were clarified: consultants will clone and adapt pilot configurations (except one pilot), develop labor standards (cleaning minutes, tasks) and incorporate special behaviors and exceptions, and request pre-work including restaurant names, meal periods, hours, and seat counts. Hotels must provide pre-work by deadlines, populate scheduling grids with staff names/availability, prioritize early-day appointments during implementations, and provide a back-of-house tour to aid standards development. Employee maintenance (hour caps, availability, preferences) is critical to reduce manual schedule edits.

System behavior, scheduling, and reporting features were demonstrated: UniFocus produces draft schedules by multiplying forecasts by labor standards and makes an initial automated scheduling pass that managers edit. The system enforces hour caps, avoids overtime and back-to-back shifts by default (overrides are possible and flagged), and displays availability indicators and draft vs. edited totals. Reporting supports scheduled automated reports, a labor dashboard for meetings, and analytics comparing forecast, recommended schedule, and actual hours. A trend example showed cases where projected labor differed from standards-based labor, highlighting forecast-to-standard gaps.

**Reporting demo — trend/analytics graph (property level)**

Ralph is walking through a trend graph (not the live system — a demo view) showing an entire month of data for one property, with the visible window defaulting to the first half of the month (1st–15th); the window is scrollable/movable in the live system to view any part of the month.

The graph shows the four labor metrics (forecast/projected, standard, scheduled/recommended, actual — per the earlier summary) as four differently-colored lines, at the **property level** — the topmost rollup. Ralph notes this is deliberately not drilled into division/department/job level yet, though that drill-down is available in the live system to isolate where over/under hours are coming from (his example: Housekeeping could be 200 hours over while Stewarding is 200 hours under, netting to a misleading property-level "balance").

He picked this specific property on purpose because its graph illustrates two distinct points cleanly:

1. **Backwards look (actual vs. standard) — this property is doing it right:** The navy blue line (**standard hours** — actual volumes × labor standards) and the green line (**actual hours worked**, employees + contract labor combined) track almost on top of each other with very little white space between them, for any day or month picked. Ralph's read: this property's managers are actively trueing up staffing to real-time conditions and consistently landing right on their labor requirement — "kudos to them."

2. **Forwards look (projected vs. standard) — the opportunity:** The pink line (**projected/forecasted labor** — same math as standard, but driven by forecasted volumes instead of actual volumes) sits noticeably lower than the navy blue standard line. Ralph is using this gap to introduce the forecast-to-standard discrepancy concept — i.e., when forecasted volumes understate what actually happens, the projected-labor line will read low relative to what the standard line (and reality) show. *(Transcript cuts off here — the point he's building to about what causes/what to do about the pink-vs-navy gap is not yet captured.)*
