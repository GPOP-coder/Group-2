## What's captured

### Ralph's closing analogy — "the treadmill"
Unifocus Planning & Scheduling is like a treadmill: buying it (installing it) doesn't help by itself — someone has to actually interact with it, or it becomes "a laundry rack" (unused, gathering clutter). Devon, Matthew, and Nicole are framed as the properties' "motivational coaches" to keep managers actually using the system, but ultimately it's on each property to engage with it.

### Future: Labor Budgeter module
- Currently used just to test standards as they're built.
- **Future plan for HM Alpha specifically:** use the Labor Budgeter to *create* labor budgets directly from the labor-standard math (e.g., time-to-clean-a-room), rather than the traditional method of taking a percentage of history.
- Rationale: history-based budgets blur job codes together (e.g., public area cleaners who always clock in as room attendants), inflating one job's hours and hiding another's. Because Unifocus enforces clocking into the correct job, it produces a cleaner real picture of where hours actually go — expected to give much better budget-to-job correlation once adopted.

### Manager training / "champion" emphasis
- Analogy: you wouldn't let an untrained front-desk hire "wing it" in Opera on their first split-folio guest, or a new server work without POS training — Unifocus deserves the same seriousness.
- Properties need a real training plan for anyone new who'll touch Unifocus, and must designate a **"champion"** at the property — and re-designate promptly if that person leaves. Ralph frames neglecting this as the direct cause of ending up with "a treadmill with laundry on it."

### Q&A — Nicole Mendez's remarks (not a question, a reminder to the group)
- **Consultants are temporary.** After the ~4-week post-training support window ends, the on-property team is on its own (consultants can be pulled back in if truly needed, but that's the exception). This makes it critical that property admins attend as many training sessions as possible now, while consultants are still actively available.
- Nicole herself has been attending trainings/configuration meetings and can help with questions, but "it's much faster if someone in the property has the answers."
- **In-software Help section is genuinely good** — detailed, searchable by keyword, has resolved her own questions without needing to contact Unifocus directly. Worth actively pointing properties to this.
- Thanked Ralph for running the kickoffs.
