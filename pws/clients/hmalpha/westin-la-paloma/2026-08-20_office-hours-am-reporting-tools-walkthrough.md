- Reports → **Labor Effectiveness Report**, filtered to F&B, run by job, for a completed week (used week ending 8/14 as the live example since current data wasn't fully populated yet).

## 3. ⚠️ Employee Schedule Analysis Report — The Drill-Down Tool

- **New report introduced, explicitly framed as a favorite: "Employee Schedule Analysis."** Not in the standard weekly labor-meeting packet — it's a diagnostic tool for exactly the question above: *"how did we get to 172 hours in that job?"*
- **Mechanics:**
  - Runs for **one job at a time** (or a whole department), for a single week, and shows **actual vs. scheduled hours per employee**, only within that specific job.
  - **Only shows hours that are "stuck" to a real Unifocus employee** — i.e., hours belonging to Western/property employees whose identity is known to the system. **Does not work for contract labor / Work Records-only hours**, since those hours land in the correct job "bucket" for reporting purposes but aren't tied to any named person the system recognizes. Banquet-driven hours were cited as another category likely to fall outside this report's reach.
  - Because no live schedules existed yet for the week examined, every line showed **S / A pairs with the schedule (S) side at zero** and only the actual (A) side populated — this is expected during the pre-go-live period, not an error.
- **Live finding using the report:** a chunk of La Vista's 172 actual hours resolved to a specific employee who had **worked the pool but was coded as a barista** — a real miscoding example, not a true overage. Left open whether this fully explains the full 61-hour gap (172 − 111) or only part of it.
- **Pete's summary framing of the tool's purpose:** *"This is not something you do in a labor meeting. It's a drill-down."* — meant for investigating a specific anomaly after the fact, not part of the standard Wednesday review cadence.
- **Action given directly:** star/favorite this report by name (**Employee Schedule Analysis**) for future use; Pete noted he specifically wants **Peggy** to see it as well, since she wasn't present for this walkthrough.

## 4. Coding Accuracy Flagged as the Real Ongoing Job (Not the Standard)

- Reiterated distinctly from [Part 4](2026-08-20_office-hours-am-budget-vs-standards-signoff.md)'s budget-vs-standard discussion: once a standard is trusted (per the ~2% rule above), the department's real recurring work becomes **making sure employees are coded to the correct job when they float between roles** (e.g., someone who's "sometimes a pool server, sometimes [something else]") — a people/process problem, not a system problem.
