## 9. ⚠️ Schedule Editor Filter Mechanics — "The Filter Is 40% of the Problems"

- **Job filter and Schedule Group filter are AND'd together, not OR'd.** If a manager filters by both a job and a schedule group and no employee matches both simultaneously, the result is empty — looks broken, isn't. Pete's fix: when someone can't see anyone in the schedule, **clear the schedule group filter first**, then troubleshoot from there.
- Filtered/sorted views can be **saved and named** — depending on permissions, saved views may be visible to other users too, so expect a growing list of views as more people build their own over time.
- Ctrl+scroll to see more of the week at once — same real-estate tip noted elsewhere.
- Open (unfilled) shifts appear in a separate panel and can be dragged onto employees — the system won't allow double-booking or assigning someone to a job they can't do.

## 11. Employee Availability — Full Live Walkthrough (Gabby Espinoza, Room Attendant)

- **Core message reinforced strongly: put something on every day, even for employees who are effectively always working.** Wide-open "all availability" isn't a convenience — it actively works against a manager, since it gives the auto-scheduler no real constraint and makes it impossible to tell, at a glance, whether an employee's record has ever been reviewed.
- **Availability is not job-specific — one single profile applies across every job an employee holds.** If an employee later picks up a second job with different hours (e.g., an overnight shift), the *same* availability window has to accommodate both; there's no way to set separate availability per job.
- **Reconfirmed: availability must be at least as wide as the actual shift the standard generates**, or the employee will simply never get that shift automatically — e.g., if the standard generates an 8:00am shift and someone's availability doesn't open until 8:30am, they will never be auto-scheduled into it.
- **Preferred Off reconfirmed yet again as commonly misunderstood** — with WLP's limited full-time staff pool relative to shift volume, the auto-scheduler will persistently try to schedule nearly everyone regardless of Preferred Off, since it only mildly deprioritizes rather than blocking.
- **Strategic point for near-always-working staff:** WLP's real scheduling flexibility lives in contract labor, not full-time Westin staff — for an employee like Gabby who's essentially always going to work, Variable + Availability alone isn't a reliable enough lever; **Regular** status is the better tool.
- **Regular status walked through live:** switching Gabby to Regular for specific days lets a manager pre-assign her a *different* time than what the standard's shift would otherwise generate (e.g., 8:30am–4:30pm instead of the standard's 8:00am shift). Regular still draws from the standard's total headcount — it does not create extra slots beyond what's budgeted — and still requires Availability to be wide enough not to conflict with the Regular-assigned time. **Practical guardrail confirmed: no more than about 4 days should be set to Regular** for one person without risking that person running out of remaining weekly hours ("running out of gas") on a later day.
- **Permanent status mechanics sharpened:** evaluated *before* both Regular and Variable in the fill sequence. Unlike Regular, **Permanent genuinely can exceed the standard's total headcount if overused** — e.g., marking 30 people Permanent for a 10-person-standard shift will schedule all 30, blowing past the standard entirely. Pete's explicit non-endorsement: **"we don't profess this as the way to go,"** though it's available and the manager remains responsible for the bottom-line numbers regardless of method.
