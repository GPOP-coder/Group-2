## 1. 🔴 Major Finding — Unmapped CI Booking-Type Field May Be Silently Dropping BEO Covers

**This is the most significant new discovery of the session**, surfaced during an extended live troubleshooting conversation between Pete and Joey Bradfield (Director of Events).

- Unifocus maps banquet KBIs from CI using **two dimensions**: the event type (plated/buffet/continental/reception/etc.) **and** a "group vs. local" (or broader booking-type) classification. Confirmed mapped booking types: **Group**, **Local**, and **In-House**.
- **Live discovery: CI's actual booking-type field contains values beyond these three** — Joey found unmapped/unrecognized options including **"Multi Year," "Rooms Only,"** and others neither Pete nor Peggy could immediately explain or locate ("What is a multi year? I don't know... I don't know if I've seen yours, but that I have [seen it elsewhere]").
- **The mechanism is silent and has no warning:** if a BEO's booking-type value doesn't match a mapped category, **its covers simply do not flow into Unifocus at all** — no error, no flag. Pete's own words: "they just like fall out of the system... sometimes you won't find out until culinary says, hey, we have this big 400-something dinner and we're not getting anything for it."
- **Nobody could identify exactly where this field lives in CI's own UI** — Joey searched live and couldn't locate it ("I can't find the field... it's built in the quote in the account somewhere in one of those fields"). Pete's suggestion: pull a full year of CI BEO history and search for any use of "Multi Year," "Rooms Only," or similar values to determine whether this is a live, real risk or a one-off/historical artifact.
- **Action item, unresolved as of this session:** Peggy and/or Joey need to (1) locate the actual field name in CI (referenced loosely as similar to Delphi's "booking type" or "source"), (2) pull historical BEOs to check for real usage of unmapped values, and (3) map any genuinely-used values to the correct group/local/in-house bucket. Until resolved, **any BEO written with an unmapped booking type is a real, undetected banquet-labor gap.**

## 2. Real Finding — Reception Sub-Type Mapping Is Incomplete and Was Fixed Live (Partially)

- WLP's CI instance only exposes **two** reception sub-types (Reception, Cocktail Reception) even though Pete has seen properties with three or four (cocktail / light / medium / heavy) — confirmed this is **controlled by Marriott/CI configuration above the property level**, not something WLP or Unifocus can add themselves ("there is a master list... someone, somewhere, has turned off or turned on... it's like, mother may I").
- Live mapping fix applied: **"Cocktail Reception" → Reception Light**, for both **Group and Local** booking types (the Local-side mapping had never been done before because CI had never previously sent that combination — "since then, we've seen it... if we would have mapped it if we had seen it, but we didn't").
- **"Reception" → Reception Heavy** (already mapped, confirmed correct).
- This reinforces the broader mapping principle: **Unifocus can only map event-type combinations it has actually seen come through from CI at least once** — a new combination appearing for the first time requires a manual mapping fix, and won't be caught automatically going forward without someone noticing.

## 3. ✅ Decision Confirmed — Coffee Break and Continuous Beverage Both Map to a Single "Break" KBI

- Live decision with Joey: rather than create a second, more granular KBI to distinguish a beverage-only break from a full coffee break (food + beverage), **both map to the same "Break" KBI**, since the labor lift (stocking, prep, stewarding, service ratio) is identical either way. Deliberately avoids KBI/mapping proliferation for a distinction that has no real labor impact.
- Directly resolves an earlier property-side frustration: **beverage-only breaks previously received no labor credit at all** ("before, they would never give us breaks for it... we finaled it by bringing green apples for wellness, that's a charge for it" — the property had been informally absorbing this gap rather than getting proper labor attribution).

## 4. Reinforced (Second Source) — DMP/Wedding Packages Are Not, and Will Not Become, CI Event Types

- Independently corroborates the same finding from the [Wednesday AM session](2026-08-19_manager-training-wednesday-am.md) §3 — this time from **Joey Bradfield, Director of Events**, describing frustration from culinary about not receiving DMP-specific labor credit.
- Confirmed explanation: DMP/wedding covers are **not missing** — they're already captured through the itemized component BEOs (breakfast, breaks, lunch, reception, etc.) that make up the package. Adding "DMP" as its own event type would **double- or triple-count** the same covers.
- Explicitly confirmed as **a CI/product decision made above Unifocus**, not a Unifocus limitation — "it wasn't Unifocus that said this... DMPs are not going to be an event type."

## 5. Standards Deep Dive — Front Desk (New Live Example)

- Full breakpoint structure demonstrated live for Front Desk Agent:
  - **7am shift:** generated whenever there is 1+ hotel departure that day, no matter what (base coverage).
  - **Phone-support shift:** added when occupancy exceeds **96 rooms**, independent of departures — driven by occupancy, not check-outs.
  - **10am shift:** added when arrivals exceed **50**.
  - **Additional 10am-window shift:** added at **170+ arrivals**.
  - **3pm shift:** generated whenever arrivals exceed 50 (separate breakpoint from the 10am logic — anticipates the evening check-in wave).
- **Live finding, not resolved:** the property's own front desk standard doesn't fully match how the department actually operates in the evening — flagged as a real discussion item for the upcoming labor meetings, not something to be silently "graded against."

## 6. Standards Deep Dive — Security (Assignment + Seasonality, Second Real Example)

- Security Officer standard: **6am, 10am, and 9am (package-delivery-specific)** shifts, with **up to 3 concurrent shifts during the day** at high volume (including the supervisor's coverage). Afternoon: a single 2pm shift most days, with the supervisor off Thursday/Friday.
- **Live finding — unpaid lunch break may not fit security's real operational constraint:** the property requires one officer in the office at all times plus one roaming, and taking an unpaid lunch creates a coverage/witness gap for radio calls or fire-alarm response. Pete's live recommendation: **build a paid/no-unpaid-lunch rule into the standard** to match real operational needs rather than forcing an unpaid break that doesn't fit the job. Not implemented live — flagged for follow-up.
- **Pool Officer** demonstrated as a **seasonal assignment** under Security Officer: zero coverage most of the year, **2 shifts on Fri/Sat/Sun only during defined date ranges** (spring ~3/1–3/15, and a longer summer window ~5/21–9/30) — a second concrete real-world example of the assignment + seasonality/subassignment pattern already documented in the AM session (bus person off-season/peak-season).

## 7. Reinforced — "Numbers at the Bottom" as Both Shield and Sword

- New framing worth preserving verbatim in spirit: running to your standard (or having a clear story for why you didn't) reframes the manager's relationship with labor from **permission** ("what am I allowed to have") to **ownership** ("here's my plan, and here's why I deviated"). Pete explicitly contrasted this with a real prior-company anecdote of a hotel refusing bell/shuttle service mid-month because department hours were "used up" — framed as guest-hostile, budget-instead-of-plan thinking that Unifocus's day-by-day/week-by-week standard is meant to replace.
- Same "shield and sword" language used in the Mohonk engagement — confirms this is a standing part of Pete's core teaching repertoire, not improvised per-property.

## 8. Real Pushback — Challenging the Assumption That Schedules Must Print Alphabetically by Name

- Live exchange with the Chef: Pete challenged the default assumption that a printed schedule needs to list every employee alphabetically. Demonstrated the **Daily Schedule Report** as an alternative — organized by **job and start time** rather than name, which better answers "who's coming in, doing what, and when" for a department head running service.
- Not a mandate — Pete's position: Unifocus **can** replicate whatever legacy printed format a department is used to, but managers should consciously choose the format that's actually useful rather than defaulting to "how it's always been done."

## 9. Contract Labor Naming Convention — Explicit Examples Confirmed

- Reinforces the placeholder/mock-employee pattern already tracked in the Tuesday PM file (Day 1 §21, Day 2 §12): explicit naming examples given — **"Room Attendant 1," "Room Attendant 2," "Cook 1," "Cook 2," "Steward 1," "Steward 2," "Steward 3."**
- Explicit rule: **never** build one contract-labor placeholder that carries multiple skill sets (e.g., one person who's simultaneously "Steward 12," a room attendant, and a banquet setup person) — each placeholder represents one job/skill only, mirroring how a real staffing-agency PO would be structured.
