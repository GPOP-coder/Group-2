## 2. KBI Types — Forecast Structure Explained in Depth

- **Input KBI:** a plain data bucket — either manually typed in or mapped to an interface (CI, ProfitSword). Nothing happens to it automatically unless it's entered or mapped.
- **Calculated KBI:** built from input KBIs via math (add/subtract/divide) — e.g., occupancy percentage. **Standing rule, reiterated with real concern below: never map an interface field directly into a calculated KBI.**
- **Primary vs. non-primary, statistical or not:** relates to the **Budgeter** tool (not yet in use at WLP — Pete flagged he'll likely work with WLP's new DOF on this once HM Alpha activates it). "Primary" KBIs are the ones that roll up into budget; granular banquet detail (plated/buffet/continental/local/group splits) generally isn't primary — only the aggregate (e.g., one single "breakfast total") typically is.
- **Market Groups:** act as independent variables for regression. **WLP uses exactly one market group, called "Hotel"** — no multi-segment rooms forecasting, consistent with WLP not using Unifocus's built-in rooms forecaster (ProfitSword remains the source of truth, per Day 1 §7).
- **Always-calculated KBIs:** arrivals, occupancy, and **departures** are always system-calculated (departures = yesterday's occupied + arrivals − today's occupied) — never manually input.
- **Revenue Center Period KBIs (dependent variables):** auto-created when a revenue center period (e.g., "Breakfast") is created — these carry special forecasting behavior, one of two types:
  - **Statistical/Regression** — recalculates a fresh "capture ratio" every week using regression. Can output **zero** if it can't find a strong enough relationship — this is *by design*, and shows up as "needs attention" warnings, expected during the first couple of months with limited history (don't mistake this for the system being broken).
  - **TAES (Trend-Adjusted Exponential Smoothing)** — Pete's plain-English gloss: **"a $500 word for a weighted average."** Always produces a number since it's averaging, never fails to zero.
- Every KBI's screen shows **what else references it** (used in a calculation, used in a standard) — a genuinely useful troubleshooting entry point for tracing why a number looks wrong.
- **Managers can't access KBI setup screens, but there is a small read-only "peek window"** (a blue "i" info icon) letting them see a KBI's formula/definition without edit rights — same mechanism referenced again in §7 below for labor standards.

## 9. Labor Standard Shift-Building Mechanics

- Standards start with **shifts**, built before any headcount rules — minimum/maximum shift length settings live here (same place the rounds-at-.2-not-.5 setting from [Day 1 §31](2026-08-17_admin-training-day1-monday.md#31-housekeeping-room-attendant-standard--full-detail-supersedes-28s-informal-version) lives) — not something to change casually.
- **Seasonal/date-driven jobs exist separately from volume-driven ones** — e.g., WLP's pool bar job is gated by calendar date (doesn't open until June) rather than any occupancy/volume driver.
- **Strict shift-naming convention used across all Unifocus properties:** `####-####  (#)` — four-digit start, dash, four-digit end, then in parentheses the **actual paid functional work hours** the shift represents (distinct from the full scheduled span once the unpaid lunch is added back in).
  - Live example: a Housekeeping Supervisor scheduled 8:30am–5:00pm (an 8.5-hour span including a 30-minute unpaid lunch) is named **"0830-1630 (8)"** — the shift itself is built as the 8-hour **functional/work** block (0830–1630); the lunch is a separate **schedule rule** that only applies once a real person is attached to the shift (i.e., a "schedule" = a person + a shift), turning it into the full 8.5-hour scheduled entry.
- Optional free-text shift notes can be added purely for internal readability (e.g., "this is the donut-prep shift") — no functional effect, not the same as an Assignment or label, doesn't surface anywhere else in the system.
- **Screen-reading tips:** collapse the Planner Settings panel to free up space, use "View Full Screen," and Ctrl+scroll to zoom — genuinely useful for anyone regularly reading standards.
- **How to read a standard's driver logic:** each shift line ties to a KBI driver with a numeric range (e.g., "1 to 90" of some KBI value generates 1 shift, "91 to infinity" generates a second, etc.) — click the small blue "i" info icon next to any unfamiliar KBI name to see its definition without leaving the standard screen.

## 16. Units-per-Shift Standards — A Second Standard Type (Housekeeping & Banquets)

- Introduced a second standard "shape" distinct from the minutes-per-unit ("pitcher and cups") model covered on Day 1/earlier today: **Units per Shift**, which thinks in ratios the way a department head naturally does — e.g., "1 [person] per 100 occupied rooms" — rather than raw minutes accumulated into shifts.
- Can vary by **day of week** — both the threshold numbers and the shift start time can differ e.g. for Sunday, on the theory that Sunday sees more departures. Pete's same recurring suggestion applied here too: if this were driven by departures instead of occupancy, day-of-week variation might not even be necessary, since departures would naturally already reflect that pattern.
- **Room Attendant standard reconfirmed via the minutes-per-unit model, with one figure worth flagging:** this session's live read stated **departures at 30 minutes per unit** — this differs from the **38 minutes** captured in [Day 1 §31](2026-08-17_admin-training-day1-monday.md#31-housekeeping-room-attendant-standard--full-detail-supersedes-28s-informal-version). Not clear whether the standard was adjusted between sessions or one of the two live reads was misheard/mis-transcribed — **flagged to reconcile against the actual live screen** rather than treated as resolved. Stayovers at 20 minutes × 85% and the 40-minutes-per-person stand-up/unproductive-time addition are consistent across both sessions.
- **"Pitcher and cups" model reconfirmed with one clarified detail:** this session states the standard produces **two short shifts every single day**, not just one as the Day 1 description implied — worth correcting forward: the model fills 8-hour "cups," and the leftover from filling both the main shift pool *and* the ~40-min/person stand-up-time pool each produce their own partial/short shift. Still by design, not a bug — those two short shifts just need to be stretched out or manually edited by the manager when scheduling.

## 17. ⚠️ Banquet Standards — Deep Detail and an Explicit, Strong Caution

- **Banquet standards are built as Units-per-Shift, but with real added complexity:** e.g., Server staffing for a plated breakfast is 1 per 35 covers; continental is 1 per 40–50; a beverage-only break is 1 per 100. **Every meal type (breakfast/lunch/dinner/reception/break) needs its own standard, separately, for every touching department** (Server, Culinary, Stewarding, Setup) — a large matrix of individual standards, not one generic banquet formula.
- **Shift length is itself a fixed assumption built into each standard, not read from the actual BEO:** breakfast is modeled as a 4-hour shift, lunch 4 hours, dinner and receptions longer (a "heavy reception" modeled longer than a standard cocktail reception) — the system never reads real BEO start/end times, so an unusually long or short real event isn't reflected.
- **"Combo shift" overlap is a known, accepted source of imprecision:** when the same staff work back-to-back events (e.g., a dinner followed immediately by a reception), each event's standard shift is modeled independently, so the combined total can **overcount** hours for that combo — a deliberate tradeoff, since under-provisioning a standalone dinner or standalone reception would be worse. Pete's term for this: banquet standards have real **"squishiness"** baked in by design.
- **⚠️ Strong, explicit caution repeated and expanded from Day 1: never scrutinize any banquet-touching schedule day-by-day** — this applies to **Server, Culinary, Stewarding, and Setup alike**, for two compounding reasons: (1) the banquet forecast is only pulled once a week (Friday) and is already stale/incomplete by the time any given day arrives, and (2) real banquet labor doesn't happen only on the event day — prep happens the day before (sometimes two days before), cleanup can spill into the next day, none of which the standard's day-of-event concentration reflects.
- **The aggregate (after-the-fact, e.g. weekly) comparison is still meaningful, even though day-by-day isn't** — if managers know their standard, they retain legitimate grounds to push back on staffing decisions (Pete's example: "we were told to staff 4 bartenders per 100, but the standard says 1 per 100" is a fair, standard-backed objection) — the caution is specifically about day-by-day accountability, not about the standard being meaningless overall.

## 19. Operating Codes (Op Codes) — Grouping Jobs That Span Departments for Reporting

- **Op Codes** let you bundle jobs that don't share a department into one reportable unit — the concrete example raised was **Security**, which isn't its own department at WLP (its Supervisor and Officer jobs currently sit elsewhere in the structure) but could be grouped into a single Op Code so it can be looked at as one line on labor reports rather than manually added up job by job.
- **Not yet built for WLP** — flagged as something Peggy will likely be the most interested party in setting up, more than anyone else on the property team.
- Some properties structure their **entire labor meeting around Op Codes** instead of the department/division hierarchy — confirmed as a legitimate, existing pattern elsewhere, not a WLP-specific suggestion yet.
- **Reports CAN be run at the Op Code level** — contrasted directly with the Assignment-level limitation confirmed in §15: Op Codes are a real reporting workaround for cross-department groupings; Assignments are not reportable at all.
