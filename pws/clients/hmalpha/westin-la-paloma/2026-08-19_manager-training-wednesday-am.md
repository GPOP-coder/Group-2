## 1. Opening Framing — "Giant Comparison Machine"

- Core teaching metaphor for the entire session: Unifocus is "a giant comparison machine... compared to what?" — every output only means something against a plan: budget, actual hours, schedule vs. plan.
- Explicitly framed as **not new** to anyone who's used LLG before — direct mapping: LLG's labor productivity factor (hours per cover in F&B, hours per occupied room elsewhere) *is* the same management practice Unifocus wraps around, just automated and disaggregated by job/day instead of one property-wide number.
- **Even "flat" departments (engineering) have a plan** — the plan just doesn't visibly change week to week the way F&B's does. A pipe breaks, someone's out, a position's open — actual vs. plan still moves, and that's still the same comparison exercise.
- This is explicitly a **management practice**, not a software feature — "the software is one thing, but it's the management practice that we wrap around it."

## 2. Weekly Planning Timeline — Full Walkthrough

- **Friday overnight:** system moves forward to next week's schedule (during this session, that's the week ending 8/28; the following Friday it rolls to 8/29–9/4).
- **Friday, rooms forecast in:** 14-day rooms forecast comes from Revenue Management (Peggy's team) automatically — no manual entry.
- **Friday, banquets in:** pulled from actual BEOs in CI (the Event Management System), not manually re-keyed. Critically, **not** bucketed by breakfast/lunch/dinner — bucketed by **service type/effort level**: plated, buffet, continental, box lunch, reception, etc., because each has a genuinely different labor lift for culinary/service/stewarding/setup. **Group vs. local is tracked separately** because group guests (sleeping in-house) don't hit the outlets the same way locals do — in-house group breakfast attendees get subtracted from projected restaurant/room-service demand ("people eat, rooms don't... sometimes 1 person per room, sometimes 4 — that matters").
- **Friday 11am–3:30pm: Generate/Edit Revenue Center Forecast.** Rooms + banquets are treated as independent/known variables; everything else (restaurant covers, room dining, bar traffic) is a **dependent variable**, calculated via either a simple average or **regression (capture ratio)** — "how many people who are in-house tonight are going to come eat, or order room service, or go to the bar." Needs ~13 weeks of clean history before regression kicks in; until then (or if the pattern is noisy), the system flags **"needs attention"** rather than guessing.
  - **Live troubleshooting principle taught here:** don't just chase "needs attention" flags — also sanity-check numbers that came back as a flat 0 or an obviously-wrong average (the Sabino's/pool-restaurant example: system generated 0, but a labor standard depends on that KBI, so it needed a real number regardless of whether it said "needs attention").
  - **Every revenue center forecast number is editable and should be overridden with local knowledge** — "we're not going back," so a wrong number here silently produces zero shifts downstream, not an error.
- **Friday, ~3:30pm: Generate Projected Hours → Generate Schedules.** This is the pivot point: "your labor standard does not schedule people, and Unifocus does not schedule people. It makes shifts. And then it tries to fill them." Auto-fill typically lands around 60% filled on first pass, not 100% — expected, not broken.
- **Friday 3:30pm → Wednesday ~2pm:** editing window. Managers fix/finish schedules but **do not publish/print** until after the Wednesday labor meeting.
- **Wednesday: Labor Meeting** (confirmed times, per Tuesday PM's session: Rooms 2:00pm, F&B 3:00pm). Two questions only: **"How did we do last week?"** and **"Are we ready for next week?"** — both explicitly framed as *comparison* questions, always "compared to your plan."
- **Wednesday, end of day: Publish.** One single person on property publishes; schedules go out via print and/or the mobile app. **Unifocus is intended to become the system of record over Excel and eventually over Paychex-entered schedules** — a Paychex-side schedule entry will eventually get overridden by what's in Unifocus, syncing overnight (with a second midday sync being added). This requirement (schedule-in-Unifocus needed to clock in) may be temporarily turned off property-wide during rollout week to avoid clock-in chaos — not fully resolved live who owns that decision (flagged to loop in "Janette").
- **Mobile app:** not mandatory yet, but the direction of travel — employees will see only *published* schedules (never draft), get notified of any post-publish change, and can be offered open/pop-up shifts to accept themselves (with manager permission gating every step — "nothing happens without your permission").
- **Time off requests:** Unifocus does **not** manage these directly — anything approved in Paychex by end of day **Thursday** is pulled in and honored in Friday's draft. Late-arriving approved requests **do not bump an already-scheduled person off** — they show as a visual flag (approved time off + still-scheduled shift) so the manager knows to manually reassign it, rather than the system silently dropping the shift into a black hole.
- **Same-day/past changes** (call-outs, replacements for a shift that already happened or is happening today): handle directly in Paychex — Unifocus doesn't need to know about anything in the past, it only cares about the current and next planning week.

## 4. KBI / Volume Architecture

- **Every single job's labor standard is driven by a KBI (volume).** No exceptions — even Executive Chef has one behind it (could be as broad as occupied rooms).
- **Two families of KBI:** (1) input/calculated KBIs (rooms, arrivals, departures, stayovers — arithmetic, not statistical), and (2) **Revenue Center KBIs**, which have "special powers" — they're the only ones that get a statistical forecast (average or regression/capture-ratio) that a manager then edits.
- **You can always see the formula behind any KBI** — click the info bubble next to it (demonstrated live on "Total Outlet PM Covers," confirmed to mean dinner covers across all outlets including room dining).

## 5. Labor Standards — Structure Deep Dive

- **A standard = a shift (time) + a KBI-driven volume threshold.** Shift names follow a `####-####` convention by default (start-end, 4 digits each) representing the working portion of the shift; the actual scheduled block adds the unpaid break on top (an "8-hour standard" becomes an "8.5-hour schedule").
- **The pitcher/cups (or "eyedropper") model, reiterated from earlier sessions:** flat/fixed departments like engineering fill whole shifts like pouring a pitcher into cups — a threshold is crossed and a full shift appears. Other jobs work more like an eyedropper — smaller, more granular volume breakpoints add labor incrementally.
- **Minimum shift length defaults to 4 hours.** Rounding logic is intentionally asymmetric: rounds up past **.2** of a shift (not the usual .5) — biased toward adding labor rather than under-crediting it.
- **Seasonality** can be applied directly to a job (an on/off date range) — same job, different operating window.
- **Assignments** are sub-jobs under a single Paychex job title that carry their *own* separate KBI and standard — e.g., "Line Cook" itself has no standard at all; the standard lives entirely on the **AM** and **PM** assignments underneath it, each with different shift times and different driving KBIs. An employee who is only assigned the base job (not the AM/PM assignment) will never be offered those shifts.
- **Subassignments go one level deeper — seasonality nested under an assignment.** Live example: Bus Person → assignment "Busser" → subassignments "Off Season" (6/1–8/15) and "Peak Season" (8/16–12/31, i.e., starting essentially the week of this training), each with its own shift times, hours of operation, and KBI breakpoints. The same structure applies to "Food Runner" under the same job. **This is the deepest, most granular real example captured this week** — worth a dedicated curriculum callout.
- **Auto-scheduler fill order, by default: top-to-bottom, left-to-right** — day 1 of the week first, then down the labor-structure job order (alphabetical unless customized), filling every eligible person's hours toward their availability cap before moving to the next slot. Configurable to prioritize the busiest/hardest day first instead, but that's not the default.

## 6. Schedule Editor — Mechanics

- **Daily coverage chart:** purple below the line = under-scheduled vs. standard; red above = over-scheduled. Small red "blips" are just unpaid-break artifacts — ignore them.
- **Filter / sort / view controls:** can filter by department/job/schedule group, hide unscheduled secondary employees (useful for reducing clutter from people who technically *could* work a shift but won't realistically be scheduled), and **save named custom views** (e.g., an "IRD schedule" view vs. a "Bar" view) so the layout persists instead of resetting every time.
- **"The numbers at the bottom" = the real comparison point** — scheduled hours vs. projected/standard hours, live-updating as you edit. **Important gotcha: the "open shifts" count does NOT decrement as you manually type in shifts from scratch** — it only reflects what the auto-scheduler originally generated and couldn't fill. Once you're manually editing, stop trusting that number and watch the bottom totals instead.
- The system **will not let you drag a shift onto someone ineligible** (already working that time, unavailable, or missing the required job/assignment) — it blocks the drop rather than letting you create a bad assignment.
- A "Show Selected KBI Data" toggle exists for visibility into the underlying volumes driving the day, but eats screen space — recommended to leave off once you trust the forecast.

## 7. Employee Maintenance — Setup Mechanics

- **First few tabs (ID, name, contact info, seniority, jobs, status) sync from Paychex** — read-only in Unifocus, not editable there. Pay rates are visible to some managers and not others (property-level decision, adjustable on request).
- **Full-time employees default to a 40-hour weekly cap** for auto-scheduler purposes, to prevent unintentional overtime generation.
- **Availability setup order matters — always set days off (Required Off) first, before setting the Available-to-Work window.** Anyone the auto-scheduler should be able to reach on a given day needs *something* entered for every day — an employee with nothing set can silently absorb shifts meant for someone else, or get skipped entirely, and the root cause is easy to misdiagnose as "the standard's wrong" when it's actually a different employee's blank/wrong availability.
- **Availability start/end times must be set wide enough to actually reach every shift the standard generates**, or that employee will never be offered certain shifts (live example: a steward whose availability started at 8am would never catch a 7am dish-room shift her standard could otherwise generate).
- **Cross-midnight shifts are a known, fiddly gotcha:** set Required Off on the non-working days first, then set Available-to-Work spanning midnight on the actual work days (e.g., 6pm–1:30am) — doing it in the wrong order or leaving a non-work day's window open produces bad results (demonstrated live, took several live corrections to get right).
- **"Sub Only" is critical for anyone with more than one job code.** Checking it hides that secondary job from the auto-scheduler entirely, so the person's *primary/home* job always gets first claim on their hours — the secondary job becomes something a manager assigns manually, not something the system offers automatically. **This is not automatic for new hires** — has to be set manually every time, a real recurring setup task.
- **Assignments must be manually added per employee, or the auto-scheduler cannot offer those shifts to them at all** — and manual dragging won't work around it either; the system blocks the assignment outright if the employee record doesn't have it. Demonstrated live with a Sabino's server (Jessica) who needed the relevant assignment added before she could receive any of her outlet's AM/PM-differentiated shifts.
- **Stale secondary job codes from Paychex will show up in Unifocus and can't be cleaned up on the Unifocus side** — if someone hasn't actually worked a given secondary job in years but it's still in Paychex, it'll still surface here. That's an HR/Paychex cleanup task, not something Unifocus controls.

## 8. Regular vs. Permanent vs. Variable — Scheduling Type Hierarchy

- **Variable** (the default/recommended starting point): the system fully picks who gets each shift, day to day, from everyone eligible. Try this first and watch what it does before overriding.
- **Regular:** locks specific people into specific recurring slots, but **still obeys the standard's headcount cap for that day** — if 10 people are set Regular but the standard only calls for 5 that day, the system still only picks 5 of them; it doesn't add capacity, just narrows who's eligible.
- **Permanent:** the strongest override — **bypasses the standard's quantity limit entirely.** The "donut shift" example: one specific person, the same shift, every day, regardless of what the standard's math says that day — use sparingly, as a last resort for a genuinely fixed exception, not a general scheduling tool.
- **Fill priority order when multiple types compete for the same shift: Permanent first, then Regular, then Variable.**
- **Recommended sequence for a manager setting this up for the first time:** start with Variable, observe a few cycles, and only escalate to Regular/Permanent for the specific slots that consistently need a named person — don't jump straight to locking everything down.

## 9. Banquet-Touching Departments — Day-by-Day Evaluation Caveat (Reinforced)

- Reiterated clearly for a mixed audience including culinary/stewarding: **any job that touches banquets (culinary prep, stewarding, event setup) should never be evaluated day-by-day**, forward or backward — the labor for a big event often lands on prep days *before* the event, not the event day itself. Only evaluate these at the **weekly total** level.
- Servers are the exception within F&B — their labor genuinely happens same-day, so day-by-day evaluation is valid for service staff even when it isn't for kitchen/stewarding.

## 10. Everything Runs in Hours, Not Dollars

- Explicit design principle: "everything we're talking about is hours... Unifocus does have dollars and can cost out a schedule... but everything is in hours." Budget functions as the property's productivity-factor equivalent from LLG; getting the hours right is what keeps the schedule on budget, not a dollars-first mentality inside the tool itself.

## 11. Weekly Labor Meeting — The Two Reports

- **Weekly Labor Summary** (backward-looking): actual vs. standard, by job and department, for the most recently *complete* week. **Read bottom-to-top and right-to-left** — the department's overall percent-over/under-standard is the headline number everyone (up to ownership) cares about first; only drill into individual job lines after that. Multi-week trend view available (e.g., 8 weeks side by side) to show whether a department is consistently over/under, not just a one-week blip.
- **Weekly Projected Schedule Report** ("the numbers at the bottom," forward-looking): scheduled vs. projected hours, by job/day, for the upcoming week — this is the "are we ready for next week" report. **Live-updates as the schedule is edited** — pulling it too early (before finishing the schedule) just shows how unfinished the schedule is, not a meaningful readiness signal. Pull it right before the meeting.
- Both reports can be filtered/summarized by job, department, or full division depending on the audience.
