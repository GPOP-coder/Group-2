# Westin La Paloma — Office Hours (Thursday AM), Part 4: Budget vs. Standards Sign-Off (BLSBD)

**Date:** August 20, 2026, AM
**Attending:** Peggy Taylor (DOF); Pete Castellano; referenced but not present: Steve Carrell, Fabrizio Poli (GM), Charles James, Mark (former DOF, retired 7/31/26 — see [property KB](kb.md#contacts)).

## 1. The Underlying Question — Did Fabrizio Actually Sign Off On the Standards?

- Steve has a checklist item to get **Fabrizio (GM)** to formally sign off that the property's labor standards were reviewed and approved — this had reportedly happened in a meeting with **Steve, Charles, and Fabrizio**, but Peggy was not part of that meeting and had not seen the underlying document herself.
- **Genuine ambiguity flagged, not resolved:** it's unclear whether signing off means *"the standards were reviewed"* or *"the standards meet budget"* — those are different claims, and there's live friction about whether the standard, as built, actually represents both the operation and the budget at the same time.
- The document in question is a **BLSBD-style PDF** — a printout/export ("Budget Labor Summary By Day") combining the labor standard settings and a job-by-job comparison against the property's budget, generated as a snapshot for sign-off purposes, separate from anything live in Unifocus.

## 2. How the Budget Feeds the Standard

- WLP's 2026 budget includes **volumes** (rooms, covers, etc.) and an **average length of stay**, from which **arrivals/departures are derived** — the budget does not directly contain arrivals/departures figures.
- **Rooms occupancy is spread through a formula that varies by day** (not flat) — daily occupancy genuinely fluctuates across the budget.
- **Everything else (F&B covers, other volumes) was brought in flat** — i.e., a single monthly covers number was divided evenly across the days of that month rather than reflecting real day-of-week variability. This is a simplification, not a data error, but it means F&B-driven standards will look smoother/less realistic day-to-day than rooms-driven ones.

## 3. ⚠️ Real Finding — Front Desk Agent Standard Is Materially Short of Budgeted Hours

- Pulled up a job-by-job budget comparison PDF (Steve's working document, downloaded locally by Pete rather than edited live).
- **Front Desk Agent: budgeted at roughly 23,000 hours for the period in question; the standard as currently built is short by roughly 2,300 hours** (~10% gap) relative to that budget figure. (Note: exact period — monthly vs. annual — was not pinned down live; treat the 23,000/2,300 figures as directionally real but confirm the underlying period before citing externally.)
- **Root cause identified live:** hours appear to have been **moved out of Front Desk Agent to accommodate two unbudgeted positions** — an added **Rooms Controller** and an extra **Supervisor** — neither of which existed as a line item in the original budget. The department's *total* hours were kept close to budget (~2% variance cited for the whole department) by robbing Front Desk Agent to fund the unbudgeted additions.
- Directly echoes the front-desk PM-shift understaffing pattern already flagged in [Day 1 §28](2026-08-17_admin-training-day1-monday.md#28-informal-standards-walkthrough--housekeeping--front-desk) and shown concretely in live schedule data in [Tuesday PM §6](2026-08-18_manager-training-tuesday-pm.md#6-schedule-editor--live-numbers-reveal-real-over-scheduling-against-standard) — this session adds the budget-side explanation for *why* front desk is structurally short.

## 4. Laundry — 20% Over Budget, Attributable to a Specific Schedule Change

- **Total laundry standard came out ~20% over budget.** Root cause: **Laundry Supervisor coverage was changed to 7 days/week in the standard, versus 5 days/week in the original budget** — effectively adding a supervisor's worth of extra days (moving from roughly 1 to potentially 3 supervisor-equivalent days across the week).
- Separately, **JW Marriott's laundry volume was not included in the original budget until March**, and its addition further inflated laundry's hours beyond what the budget anticipated. Confirmed as a real, explainable variance, not an error.

## 5. F&B Admin — 100% Over an Essentially Nonexistent Budget

- **No F&B Admin line existed in the budget at all**, and there is currently no F&B Admin labor standard either — so any hours logged there register as effectively infinite/100% over "budget." Not flagged as a problem requiring immediate action, just noted as a gap in the comparison document (can't be over a budget that was never set).

## 6. Where This Leaves the Sign-Off

- **Not resolved by end of session.** Peggy did not have Steve's BLSBD comparison document herself; Pete located and reviewed a downloaded copy live but did not make any changes to it.
- **Open, unresolved tension restated plainly:** it's fine for a property to exceed its budget in a given job/department **as long as it's documented and explained** (Rooms Controller/extra Supervisor at Front Desk; extra Laundry Supervisor days; unbudgeted JW Marriott laundry volume) — the sign-off's real purpose is making sure Fabrizio has seen and accepted those specific variances, not a blanket "standards = budget" attestation.
- **DOF continuity gap noted implicitly:** this entire budget was built under the prior DOF (Mark, retired 7/31/26) with only "very little" direct involvement from him in the original budget meetings — Peggy is now trying to reconstruct the reasoning behind variances she wasn't present for.
- **Same afternoon, on the 2:00 PM HMAlpha weekly call, Pete separately confirmed this is really Fabrizio's (not Peggy's) hesitation** — Ralph had spoken with Fabrizio directly ~30 min before that call. Fabrizio's actual concern: prior experience with 5 different labor-management systems taught him that consultant-built-from-zero standards were reliably good, but he's cautious about signing before seeing real week-over-week performance, and worried sign-off might mean standards can't be revisited later. Ralph/Devon's clarification: signing off does not freeze the standards — minor tuning remains Unifocus's responsibility through post-go-live follow-up; only a full standards rebuild would need a separate scoped engagement.
