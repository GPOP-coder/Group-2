# Westin La Paloma — Office Hours (Thursday AM), Part 3: Finance Dept Scheduling Walkthrough & Contract Labor Policy

**Date:** August 20, 2026, AM
**Attending:** Peggy Taylor (DOF), working through her own Finance-department staff scheduling; Pete Castellano.

## 1. Core Method Reinforced: Match Employee Maintenance to the Standard

- Recurring principle stated explicitly and applied to every example below: **"Know your standard first, then make your employee maintenance match the standard, and they will catch these shifts."** If availability is wide open or doesn't match the standard's generated shift times, the auto-scheduler distributes shifts semi-randomly among whoever's eligible rather than to the "right" person.

## 2. Example — Pam Thornton (Staff Accountant, 6:30am–3pm, Mon–Fri)

- Standard generates 6:30am shifts Monday–Friday; Pam's availability needed to be **Required Off Saturday/Sunday**, then **Available to Work 6:30am–3pm Monday–Friday** to reliably catch her own shifts instead of competing with other staff accountants for the same 6:30am slot.

## 3. Example — John McKinnon (Staff Accountant, 8am–4:30pm, Tue–Sat)

- Same pattern: **Required Off Sunday/Monday**, **Available to Work 8am–4:30pm Tuesday–Saturday**, matched against an existing 8am standard for that role.

## 4. Staff Accountant Headcount

- Confirmed WLP runs **four (4) staff accountants**, all clustered around the same 6:30am standard start time, plus **one payroll specialist** — five people total contending for essentially the same handful of standard-generated shifts before availability windows were narrowed to split them apart correctly.

## 5. Example — Payroll Specialist (2 Days/Week)

- Standard for Payroll Specialist only generates shifts on the two days actually worked (Monday/Tuesday in the walkthrough).
- Job is marked **Sub Only** for other jobs the same person can flex into (e.g., banquet server) — meaning she's eligible but won't be auto-scheduled into those roles.
- **Practical guidance reinforced:** even when someone works only 2 days, **block every other day explicitly** (Required Off) rather than leaving them blank — consistency matters because a blank day looks, to anyone reviewing the schedule later, like it was never considered at all. Pete's framing: *"we told everybody, put something on every day... at least put something on every day so we know that you thought about it."*

## 6. Contract Labor — Decision Explained for the First Time to Peggy

- Peggy raised: Steve had mentioned "setting up contract labor" the day before — this session was the first time the actual mechanism was walked through with her directly.
- **Confirmed policy (industry/legal framing given directly by Pete):** contract labor employees should **never** appear on the schedule under their real names, and various properties differ on this, but Pete was explicit about the risk: *"if you're treating them like an employee, then at some point they become an employee, whether you like it or not."* Putting a real, ongoing contract worker on the schedule under their own name for 40 hours/week starts to look like co-employment for benefits purposes.
- **Correct pattern: build placeholder/mock "jobs," not placeholder people with multiple jobs.** If one contract worker (referred to generically as "Jim" in the walkthrough) covers 10 different roles across a week, WLP does **not** build one mock employee named for that person with 10 job assignments — instead, WLP builds generic numbered placeholders per role (e.g., "Room Attendant Contract Labor 1, 2, 3...") and schedules against those.
- **These placeholders are not entered in Paychex at all** — they exist only in Unifocus for scheduling purposes. Actual hours for contract labor come in separately via **Work Records**, in bulk (e.g., "400 hours" or "600 hours" dumped in at once from the staffing agency), and are **actuals/time-and-attendance only** — not tied to the placeholder schedule entries or to any real employee record.
- **Explicit reconciliation gap acknowledged as WLP's own future problem, not Unifocus's:** matching which placeholder slot corresponds to which real contract worker (for payroll/agency invoice reconciliation) is something WLP will need to manage on their own — Pete's summary: *"how you're matching up who that person is on the schedule versus who a real person is from Paychex or Work Records, your agency or whatever — our advice is, don't put the name on Unifocus... whatever you do after that, we've said what we're gonna say."*
- This is the same underlying decision already reconfirmed as WLP's settled direction across departments in [Tuesday PM §4](2026-08-18_manager-training-tuesday-pm.md#4--contract-labor-entry--decision-confirmed-for-all-departments) — this session is the first time it was explained end-to-end (schedule vs. Paychex vs. Work Records) to Peggy specifically.
