- **Issue #1 — actuals mismatch:** Unifocus is correctly displaying whatever ProfitSword sends it (confirmed matching, e.g. ProfitSword's 62 = Unifocus's 62) — but ProfitSword's own numbers don't match Oracle/Opera's actual covers/departures. Not a rounding issue: gaps of **20–50 covers**, not 2–3.
  - **Devon's read (95% confident):** a mapping issue on ProfitSword's side — the PMS/POS import codes aren't all mapped to the correct account/stat inside ProfitSword. She's already exchanged screenshots with ProfitSword support (Christine) and has tickets open with **both ProfitSword and Oracle**. Offered to join a call with the property if needed to help find the unmapped codes directly.
- **Issue #2 — no arrivals/departures/guest forecasting happening at all:** per Devon, HMAlpha's revenue managers (with one property-level exception) only forecast **occupied rooms** — never arrivals, departures, or guest counts. Whatever numbers appear in ProfitSword for those fields are just **carried-over prior-year actuals** used as a budget starting point, not real forecasts, and nobody is updating them.
  - Some GMs separately run their own informal 14-day forecast outside ProfitSword/Unifocus — inconsistent across properties, not centrally owned.
  - **Decision:** keep the ProfitSword-to-Unifocus forecast import active for every property regardless of whether that property actually enters real numbers in ProfitSword — so behavior is consistent portfolio-wide (no property missing the import). Properties should still be encouraged to enter real forecast numbers in ProfitSword directly (since they're already forecasting occupied rooms there anyway), but even a stale/carried-over number is treated as a better starting point than blank, as long as the property reviews and adjusts it in Unifocus weekly.
- **Process guidance reaffirmed:** training always directs properties to at least review the imported forecast weekly (can be delegated as low as front office manager level) — the forecast side is low-stakes to fix. **The actuals mismatch is the higher-stakes problem**, since it directly undermines confidence in labor-standard variance reporting and labor meetings if managers start questioning whether their "over/under standard" numbers are even accurate. Ralph reinforced: **data validation in the first two months, before trusting variance reports or having tough conversations with managers about over/understaffing, is essential** — this is a portfolio-wide practice, not unique to Charlotte.
