## 1. Labor Standards vs. Budget — Core Tension
- James's original standards (built Aug 2024) were a first pass; ~20% of the full revision list James sent has been actually adopted so far — some standards are stale given operational changes since.
- James's principle: standards should reflect what he's actually staffing (which is dictated by operational reality, e.g., buffet needs 5, standard says 3.5), not the other way around — he won't understaff to hit a stale standard.
- Counter-concern (Lou/Pete): raising standards to match reality that's already over budget just locks in the overage — need to verify against budget, not just actuals.
- Reconciling principle agreed: **live by the budget as the primary constraint; use Unifocus to diagnose *where* variance is happening**, then decide standard-by-standard whether to adjust.
- YTD F&B payroll variance: ~$10,000 over on a ~$1.5M base — small in percentage terms.
- Multi-year goal: move from history-based staffing guides (no variability) to a standards-driven budget, so budget becomes an output of volume × standard rather than a separate manual exercise.

## 5. Kids Buffet — Uncaptured Driver
- Daily Kids Buffet (running since mid-June) adds **~12 hours/day** (2 people) not reflected in any current budget or standard.
- Buffet has also structurally changed since 2024 (needs 4 attendants/day now vs. 3 originally).
- Seasonal pattern: daily in peak summer; weekend-only in shoulder/off-season, roughly triggered by a **30+ kids threshold**.
- Agreed: standards and next year's budget both need explicit Kids Buffet line items with seasonal triggers built in — currently a real, unbudgeted gap, not standards drift.

## 6. Server vs. Support-Role Cost Strategy
- James's ongoing multi-year push: shift hours from servers (~$27–30/hr) toward server assistants/bussers/runners (~$18–20/hr) — same guest service, lower cost.
- ADP job coding already separates function-level costs (e.g., a server covering host duty codes to host labor), which helps isolate true role costs even when staff cross-cover.
- Directional agreement: tighten the waiter/waitress standard, increase runner/support-role standards, aim for a roughly cost-neutral swap in total dining room labor dollars.

## 7. Scheduling Practice — Overscheduling + Roll Call
- Standard predicted ~2,800 hrs; James scheduled ~3,600 (intentionally over, to absorb call-outs); actual landed ~3,000.
- Mechanism: **on-call shifts + roll call** (2 hrs before shift, via Beekeeper) — cut down to need, or send people home if overstaffed.
- Pete's read: overscheduling is fine and should stay **outside the standard**, not baked into it — the standard should represent true need; overscheduling is a separate, intentional buffer that the on-call system already manages well.

## 10. Main Dining Server/Runner Standard Redesign (worked live in the session)
- Current logic: 1 server per 25 covers, **plus a fixed +2** above 180 covers — the +2 was originally a scheduling-era artifact per Pete, but James maintains it represents real floater/assist capacity baked into how the room has always run (not padding).
- Target-backed exercise: James's actual practice tops out at **17 servers around 400–425 covers** for breakfast — worked backward to re-derive the ratio from that real ceiling rather than an arbitrary fixed add-on.
- Result tested live: replaced "1-per-25 + fixed 2 above 180" with "1 + 1-per-25" (a 2-server floor that scales continuously) — nets out close to current practice but removes the discontinuous jump at 180 covers. James pushed back that this still functionally cuts a server at breakfast; flagged to reconcile at Wednesday's follow-up session.
- **Patrice's point:** cover count alone doesn't determine staffing need — table mix (deuces vs. large parties) and station count matter just as much, especially at lower volumes where the room still needs enough open stations to reach every seated group within the service window. Not fully resolved live; flagged as its own follow-up.
- For dinner, discussed moving from flat 1-per-15/25 to a **volume-based sliding ratio** (~1:12 when slow, ~1:15 when busy) with breakpoints, to approximate seasonality (deuces-heavy winter vs. large-party-heavy summer) without building full seasonal date logic. Pete set up a working example live; breakpoints are placeholders, not final.
- **Pete made a backup copy of the standards before changing anything** (explicit ask from Lou/James to preserve the "before" state for comparison).
