Concern: updating labor standards could push payroll above budget; avoid changes that require reversal. Current standards are ~20% of the full list (built August 2024) and some are outdated; goal is aligning with actual staffing levels without inflating headcount. Standards not reflecting reality cause inaccurate weekly reports (e.g., buffet needs 5 staff, standard shows 3.5). Staffing guides are history-based and lack variable standards — moving to standards-driven budgeting is a multi-year goal. YTD payroll variance: ~$10,000 over on a $1.5M base (small percentage). **Agreement: live by the budget as the primary obligation; use Unifocus to diagnose where variances occur; review standards carefully against operations with budget impact as a key constraint.**

### 2. Data Integrity and Reporting Gaps

### 3. Live Unifocus Review — Food & Beverage
Overall F&B labor standards 8% higher than actual hours for week ending 7/26 — standards may be above operational reality. By department: Founders 24% over standard; Main Dining 7–9% over (FOH focus); Backwoods Beverage significantly under; Conference Services on standard. ### 4. Main Dining Room Variance by Role
Buffet attendants ~50% over; captains ~17% over; employment coordinator under; hosts on target; food runners over; servers under. Trainer hours lumped into server; debate on backing out ~40 training hours from actuals. Anomaly: one employment coordinator showing 47 vs. 40 hours for a 40-hour week. **Conclusion: overages concentrated in buffet/busser and runner roles; servers under budget.**

### 5. Kids' Buffet and Buffet Standards
Since mid-June, daily Kids' Buffet adds ~12 hours/day, not reflected in current budget or standards. Buffet configuration changed (needs increased, e.g. 3 to 4 attendants/day). Seasonal variability: daily in peak summer, weekend activation in shoulder/off seasons (e.g. 30+ kids threshold). Standards and next year's budget must be updated to account for Kids' Buffet and revised buffet staffing.

### 6. Strategy: Servers vs. Support Roles
Shift toward more server assistants, bussers, and runners at lower hourly rates to reduce cost. Payroll mapping challenges: food runner pay grouped with server on reports; ADP job codes allocate costs by function even when higher-paid staff cover lower-paid roles. Senior servers ~$27–30/hour; support roles ~$18–20/hour; cost savings by favoring support roles. Action direction: tighten waiter/waitress standards, increase runner/support standards to match operations and savings.

### 7. Scheduling Practices and Cutting to Actuals
Unifocus shift standards used for precise scheduling and overtime control. Practice: intentional overscheduling with on-calls due to callouts; hours cut at roll call (e.g., schedule 3,600, cut ~600, actualize ~3,000 vs. standard ~2,800–3,000). If scheduled too close to standard, callouts risk understaffing. Structured on-call/roll-call process absorbs volatility; opportunity to reduce overscheduling or cut more aggressively to land closer to need.

### 8. Forecasting Accuracy and Standards Validation
8-week trend generally within ±10%, mostly under 5% except during banquet-contamination periods. Need better meal-level forecasting (breakfast/lunch/dinner) to align scheduled hours with demand. Validate standards after removing banquet noise, using complete period data (June and Period 4 ending July 19, 2026).

### 9. Beverage Labor and Reporting Structure
Beverage labor isolated from dining; post-separation runs consistently under standard (last week ~90% under budget). Historical bundling of banquet beverage into "bartender" addressed; new coding by venue (Sunset, Carriage Lounge, Dining Room Bar) implemented. Plan to permanently divide beverage from dining in reporting and P&L for clarity.

### 10. Interaction Between Beverage and Dining Staff
Bartenders generally do not support dining tables. When servers are sent to Carriage Lounge, they are coded to that outlet. Hours attribution "squishiness" improved via departmental isolation and coding.

### 11. Redesign of Dining Server/Runner Standards
Current standards use units per shift (e.g., 1 server per 25 covers); breakfast uses short shifts. Prior rule added a fixed "+2" servers above 180 covers — seen as a scheduling-era artifact by some, integral float capacity by others. Proposal: remove fixed over-scheduling logic, keep variable standard with a 2-server minimum, consider minutes-per-unit for smoother rounding. Target: reach ~17 servers just below peak covers (~400–425 covers at breakfast). Clarify cover-driven staffing vs. station staffing to prevent overstaffing when covers don't require full stations. Parallel proposal: 1 server per 25 covers breakfast/lunch, 1 per 15 dinner; adjust mix to add runners/bussers while reducing servers to hold total labor cost flat.

### 12. Seasonality and Volume-Based Standards
Peak season more efficient (large parties); shoulder/winter seasons increase labor per cover, more "deuces" reducing per-server cover counts. Current system applies one standard year-round; seasonal standards would require setup work. Agreement to test a dynamic, volume-based ratio (e.g., 1:12 slow, 1:15 busy) rather than fixed seasonal standards. Acknowledge variability from banquets, occupancy, and weather requiring meal-by-meal adjustments.

### 13. Department and Job Structure Reorganization
Beverage departments previously broken out by outlet need reconsolidation to match GL structure. Op codes pre-built to isolate areas (e.g., FOH main dining). Further job-level reclassification planned, requires time.
