## 1. Duplicate Scheduling Systems (Unifocus + Legacy Excel/Macros)
- Several departments enter schedule data into Unifocus but then rebuild a separate printable version in Excel or custom macros, because Unifocus's print output doesn't support features they rely on (color-coding, specifically).
- Confirmed examples: **David (Call Center)** has built an elaborate custom macro that pre-applies breaks/lunches to the Unifocus-derived schedule; **Stacey (Front Desk)** described as "pretty wild," her own macro-driven rework; **Housekeeping** also maintains a parallel printed version.
- **Plan:** hold a **combined "schedule printing" training session** for all departments running dual systems, after the current onsite week — likely in the **Parlor/Auditorium** given the number of attendees. Not scheduled to a specific date yet.
- **Pete's framing for that session:** printing the raw Unifocus schedule alphabetically and posting it in the Employee Dining Room doesn't actually help staff find their shift — "that spreadsheet is not for the employees." If the printed output needs to double as the employee-facing schedule, it needs to be organized usefully (by department/time), not just technically complete.
- Not positioned as a mandate to kill the legacy tools — the goal is that once the training closes the capability gap, departments migrate voluntarily.

## 2. Housekeeping — Public Area Attendant (PAA)
- **Gap identified:** projected hours 128 vs. actual staffed hours 176 — a ~38% overage.
- **Standard:** AM shift + PM shift always generate regardless of volume; a weekend-only flex shift adds when the previous day's occupied-room count exceeds 150.
- **What's driving it:** primarily outdoor bathrooms — Sports Center, Granary, and the beach (seasonal, weather-dependent, no covers/guest-count KPI exists or is planned for it) — which get cleaned on a fixed schedule regardless of actual usage, since staff can't tell in advance how heavy the use will be and the areas are open to anyone walking by, not just registered guests.
- **Open question raised, not changed:** should the "busy" trigger also apply on weekdays following a high-occupancy day, not just weekend mornings following a busy Saturday? **Decision: keep the current weekend-only trigger, revisit later with more data** — deliberately deferred, not dismissed.

## 3. Housekeeping — Grove Lodge Coverage
- Grove Lodge is a standalone 6-room building, covered by a dedicated attendant who also handles gatehouse and golf shop bathrooms on the same daily run (drives a golf cart to all three stops) — coded as a **fixed daily support shift, no matter what**, separate from the volume-driven Room Attendant standard.
- Room-cleaning hours for Grove Lodge's 6 rooms scale under the regular **Room Attendant** occupied-room count — the 6 rooms are included in the property's total occupied-room number, **not broken out as a separate KPI**. When Grove Lodge is fully occupied, 2 room attendants get dispatched, drawing from the same standard Room Attendant pool.
- **Decision: leave the current coding as-is** — breaking Grove Lodge out as its own KPI was discussed and explicitly judged premature/over-engineering ("getting too fancy") given the small scale (6 rooms).

## 4. Housekeeping — Room Attendant Productivity Model ("the cups")
- **Standard:** 40 minutes per departure + 20 minutes per stayover + 40 ancillary minutes per room attendant, summed and distributed into 8-hour "cups" (shifts).
- **Known side effect:** because the system fills a cup, then starts a fresh cup for leftover minutes rather than topping off a partial one, it can generate **short/partial shifts** that aren't meant to be worked as scheduled — schedulers (specifically **Nancy**) are trained to either delete them, reassign the hours into a real full shift, or expand them into full shifts.
- **Likely connection:** this pattern may be part of what's been showing up in Nancy's call-out/scheduling complaints — worth raising directly with her at **tomorrow's training session** (Thursday AM Basic Training).
- **Context, not a problem:** full-time year-round staff expect a 40-hour week; friction during low-volume stretches was a real issue earlier in the year, improved recently after reducing headcount toward a more seasonal mix.
