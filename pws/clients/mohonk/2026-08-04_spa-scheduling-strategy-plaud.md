## AI Meeting Notes (Plaud summary, in full)

### Current use of scheduling and booking systems (Book4Time, DataVision, Unifocus)
Treatment professionals aren't scheduled in Unifocus; a year-out open book sells appointments. Existing bookings live in Book4Time, which opens availability for guests and isn't being replaced by Unifocus. Continued pre-scheduling for spa/banquets where advance demand is known. DataVision is in the data flow; Unifocus consumes files from DataVision. **Conclusion: continue using Book4Time for advance scheduling — Unifocus won't replace it, and can generate projected hours/schedules without disrupting existing bookings.**

### Spa performance, volume metrics, and data availability
Spa generates ~$6M annually (vs. ~$2.2M resort-spa average). Reliable current KBI for forecasting is Total Treatments from Book4Time; the other three (day/transient, overnight, employee treatments) weren't working in Unifocus as of this meeting. **Agreed: use Total Treatments as the primary workload driver** since labor doesn't meaningfully differ by treatment type/length for now; revisit granularity once DataVision availability is clearer.

### Service offerings and capacity
Massage, facials, body treatments, nail services; hair being phased out. 16 treatment rooms (1 couples, 1 seasonal outdoor), up to ~16 simultaneous treatments (~20 including salon). Up to 90 appointments/day.

### Scheduling philosophy vs. labor comparison standards

Interest in benchmarking provider hours to standards for comparison, not control. **Conclusion: keep operational overscheduling as-is; measure success via actual vs. standard in Unifocus.**

### Revising labor standards, seasonality, and reporting cadence
Fixed standards appear high relative to actual use; suggested lowering baseline (~380 hrs in summer) with variability. Seasonal adjustments expected (colder weather = higher baseline); weather-driven spikes year-round.
