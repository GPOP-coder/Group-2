## 2. Weekly Forecasting/Scheduling Cycle — Recap for This Group

Pete walked the same weekly-cycle Excel/whiteboard sequence covered with Monica on 8/31 (see companion file), re-taught for this audience:

- Work week planning is forward-looking. Active planning week at time of session: **9/6–9/12**. On Thursday (9/3), the system moves forward to **9/13–9/19**.
- Sequence: rooms forecast + banquet forecast entered → **Generate Revenue Center Forecast** (Thursday) → F&B revenue-center edit window through **Friday noon** → **Generate Projected Hours + Generate Schedules** (Friday noon, hard cutoff — Pete: *"I will be on a plane, so wish you well"*) → managers edit/build schedules Friday–Wednesday → **Wednesday labor meeting** (schedule must be done and defensible) → publish.
- Rooms don't eat, people do — banquet/outlet forecasting starts from **guest counts**, not occupancy; a full house could be one guest per room or several per room depending on group type (Pete's example: a dance-competition pageant group, drawing on his own "dance dad" background, could run 4–5 guests/room).
- Labor meeting purpose reiterated: *"We're answering two questions: How did we do last week, and are we ready for next week?"* Schedule-as-PO framing: the schedule is effectively a labor purchase order that the labor meeting approves.
- Contrasted explicitly against LG's rigid productivity-only math: *"It's not a productivity number. We tie it back to a productivity number when we're done... You still have a certain number of cooks you need in the kitchen no matter what."*
- **Publish mechanics:** "Publish" is specifically the trigger for the mobile app — employees can't see schedule edits until publish happens; printing (Excel/PDF/WhatsApp/etc.) can happen anytime regardless of publish status. Recommended practice: one single publish deadline for the whole property, not staggered by department.

## 7. Schedule Editor Mechanics

- **Filter behavior (repeat of the footgun flagged 8/31):** Schedule Group and Division/Department/Job filters are independent and **AND-combined** — an unhelpful combination shows nobody and reads as "broken." Pete recommends starting with one job at a time while learning.
- **Coffee cup icon:** appears when a shift crosses 6 hours, automatically adding a 30-minute unpaid lunch (schedule lunch rule). A shift + a person = a "schedule," which is what triggers the rule.
- **Manager icon (a small headshot):** flags any shift that was manually added or edited by a person, vs. system-generated and untouched.
- **"Numbers at the bottom" is the operative metric**, not who's on the grid or when: scheduled vs. projected shifts/hours, by day and in total, is what shows up in labor-meeting reports — names and time slots on the editor screen are working detail nobody else reviews.
- **Open shifts don't disappear** when you schedule manually instead of dragging system-generated ones — they simply stop mattering; only the bottom-line totals matter.
- Live demo numbers Pete read off the sample schedule: *"It made twelve shifts. I filled two of them on Sunday. On Monday, it made sixteen shifts. I filled none of them... it made one hundred thirty-one shifts. I've scheduled two of them. It made eight hundred twenty-one hours. You've scheduled fifteen and a half."*

## 9. Overscheduling for Banquets — Expected, Not to Be Flagged

- Culinary/stewarding/anything touching banquets should expect to run **overscheduled on the day of a large event** and underscheduled around it — because prep and teardown labor gets spent on adjacent days, not concentrated on the event date itself. Pete: *"In banquets you can't look at a single day... anything that touches banquets you should be allowed to overschedule, knowing that the hours will come in later."*
- Explicit contrast with the old LG-style single-day productivity math, which had no way to represent this and would just read as "off."
- Banquet visibility limit reiterated: Unifocus only knows about banquets as far out as 16 days (the prior Thursday's BEO pull) — anything closer-in is invisible to the forecast until it shows up in actuals.

## 10. Third-Party / Contract Labor — Placeholder "Temp" Employees

- Contract/agency labor should **not** be entered under real names on the schedule — Pete's stated reasons: joint-employer risk, and it's simply not accurate since the specific agency worker filling a shift isn't guaranteed. Convention: placeholder employees with last name **"Temp"** and first name matching the job (e.g., "Temp, Cook A/B/C...") — one placeholder per distinct job the person could fill, not one flexible placeholder covering multiple jobs.
- **Abdellah pushed back with a real operational concern:** he already knows his regular third-party culinary staff by name and skill, and will need to have a separate conversation with his agency contact about how the "Temp 1 / Temp 2" convention will actually work day-to-day. Unresolved — flagged for follow-up outside this session. Pete acknowledged the placeholders aren't even built yet for this property: *"Right now we don't even have the temps in there."*
- What matters on the schedule screen for contract labor specifically: scheduled hours vs. projected hours at the bottom, functioning like an approved PO against which actual agency hours (via work records) later reconcile.
