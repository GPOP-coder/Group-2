## Three-Part Training Structure

Akram's methodology has three integrated components:

### Part 1: Audit Setup + Live Configuration
- **Live demonstration** of configuration tasks in front of trainees
- Real examples: task scheduler, automation, report scheduling
- Shows *how* things work while *doing* them
- Positioning: "Here's an example of how you can automate reporting"
- Combines: audit findings + implementation + training simultaneously

### Part 2: Dashboard & Reporting Storytelling
Focus on **four core metrics** and how to interpret them:

#### The Four-Metric Framework (Critical PWS Deliverable)

| Metric Pair | Question Being Answered | Represents |
|---|---|---|
| **Projected vs. Scheduled** | Are we planning the schedule to match our forecast? | Planning accuracy (beforehand) |
| **Standard vs. Actual** | Are we delivering labor as planned? | Execution accuracy (after the fact) |

**Key insight:** Trainees struggle to absorb these four simple metrics. They need visual reinforcement.

**Akram's infographic approach:** 2x2 matrix (like Eisenhower Matrix)
- Horizontal axis: Scheduled (left) / Actual (right)
- Vertical axis: Standard (top) / Projected (bottom)
- Each quadrant tells a different operational story

**Implementation note:** Pete to create polished infographic; Akram will share working draft when perfected.

### Part 3: Scenario-Based Analysis
Walk trainees through real scenarios using the four metrics:
- "What story do you build when you compare Projected to Scheduled?"
- "What question are you answering when you compare Standard to Actual?"
- Frame as decision-making tool for operations teams

## Training Delivery Philosophy

### Standardized vs. Personality-Driven

**The Problem:** Training cannot depend on a single trainer's "brand" or flair.
- **Anti-pattern:** "The Paul Show" (reference: Paul Alexander, former Unifocus trainer, had memorable style but created dependency)
- **Goal:** Replicable structure that works whether Pete trains, Taylor trains, or a new trainer trains

**Why it matters:**
- Ensures consistency across HM Alpha portfolio
- Prevents consultant burnout ("I don't want to be doing this all the time")
- Allows other consultants to pick up where you left off
- Scales to new trainers without losing quality

**Akram's objective:** "Whether you're training or a new person trains, they have a general structure for how to approach training."

## Training Session Structure

### Session Consolidation Strategy
- **Recommendation:** Force all staff into single session when property is small (<15 staff)
- **Rationale:** Better engagement, stronger culture impact, avoids training same material 2-3 times
- **Exception:** 24-hour operations may require multiple sessions for coverage

### Training Duration Model
- **3-day training** often requires 4-5 day travel commitment (dead time)
- **4-day training** often requires 5+ days (consulting inefficiency)
- **Ralph's focus:** Bank additional billable days by tightening training windows
- **Reality check:** John completed implementation in 2 days (property assigned 5). Model is compressible.

### Last Day: Flexible Office Hours
- If trainees are behind: Schedule additional sessions to catch up
- If follow-up implementation work needed: Same consultant does it (continuity)
- If consultant can leave early: Leverage earlier flights home
- **Philosophy:** Structured but flexible based on actual need

### Admin Training
- Separate from end-user training
- Break into 2-3 hour sessions as needed (property preference)
- **Timing note:** Week-before scheduling is inefficient; coordinate closer to date

## Status Letters / Recap Notes

### Purpose
Document what was accomplished, what's pending, and any blockers at end of visit.

### Required Content
- **Accomplished:** What was completed during this visit
- **Pending:** What remains for next visit or follow-up
- **Blockers/Issues:** Any obstacles encountered

### Delivery
- Sent to HM Alpha stakeholders
- Consultants cc'd on responses
- Milestone-based (not daily)
