## Key Facts Confirmed On the Record

### ASARCO
- American Smelting and Refining Company
- Operated ~120 years on the Missouri River just north of downtown Omaha
- One of the most significant Superfund cleanup projects in U.S. history — lead and arsenic contamination across East Omaha
- Smokestack demolished 1997; site remediated and redeveloped in following years
- Daub called it the **linchpin**: *"If we hadn't have done that, none of what you see today would be happening."*
- Deal: hold-harmless from environmental liability → city receives property title + **$50 million check**
- Negotiated in two weeks; Nebraska statutes mirrored federal environmental law, giving Daub legal leverage

### Aaron Ferer & Sons
- Long-established Omaha metal trading and scrap processing firm, 155 Ida Street
- Daub secured their riverfront property after getting ASARCO title
- Harvey Fair (Ferer) had told Daub: *"Great idea, Mayor. It'll never happen. Come back and talk to me when you get title to ASARCO."*
- Daub called him on a Sunday afternoon with the title in hand

### Union Pacific
- Shop site also contaminated (oil, solvents, grease from equipment repair)
- Deal brokered over lunch at the football club with Drew Lewis and Dave Sokol
- UP moved shops to North Platte; gave city title in exchange for hold-harmless on pollution suits

### Six Development Agreements
- Back to the River covered South Omaha rail yards all the way to Epley Airfield
- Full infrastructure rebuilt underneath: sewers, water mains, drainage
- Total project cost: ~$1 billion (not counting the bridge)
- Trees cannot be planted in the remediated area — roots would penetrate impermeable contamination barriers

### The Bridge Funding
- Daub directed Pete Castellano to the **Alternative Pathways Trust Fund** — a sub-trust of the federal Highway Trust Fund
- A footbridge qualifies as an alternative pathway (same source NRD used for trail funding)
- Pete brought Senator Kerrey into the funding effort
- Federal funds: $22 million (started at $18M; grew with extras)
- Three major private corporate contributors; ~$75 million public-private partnership for the broader project
- Architect: HNTB (HDR did not get the contract)

### City Council Vote — ASARCO 4–3
- Mark Crapser held out; called Daub Sunday night before the vote
- Deal: MECA board rotating appointments — mayor and city council alternate
- Daub agreed without hesitation; Crapser kept his word
- That governance structure for MECA stands today

### Gallup Campus
- Jim Clifton (CEO) showed up 7:30 AM unannounced; Gallup was leaving Nebraska
- Had land in Princeton NJ; signed lease at Dulles Airport
- Would stay only if Omaha delivered ~23 acres on the river next to the convention site
- Daub: *"Good news is we don't have 31 acres for you. Good news is we have 30. We have to move a bridge."*
- Clifton wanted a decision in 24 hours; letter of agreement delivered to Daub's office the next morning
- Lincoln Mayor Don Wesley was furious

## The Pete Castellano Exchange (Verbatim — corrected from recording)

*Near the end of the interview. Personally significant — on the record.*

**Pete:** "I'm the only person who can say I've worked for both of you."

**Daub:** "You did, you worked for both of us. Boy, did I get shit for hiring you."

**Pete:** "I bet."

**Daub:** "Boy did I get crap..."

**Pete:** "But I got it too."

**Daub:** "For going to work for me?"

**Pete:** "But I was happy to do it. I thought it was a smart move on my part."

**Daub:** "It was a smart move on my part. Your wife thought so too. She thought it was OK."

**Pete:** "She gave up a job going to Seattle."

**Daub:** "I know, I know."

**Pete:** "Washington. She was like, 'We're going to stay right here 'cause you are working for the Mayor.'"

**Background:**
Kerrey's Senate term ended. Incoming Senator Ben Nelson (former Nebraska governor) chose to bring in most of his own state staff, taking only one or two Kerrey people. Kerrey supported Nelson, and Kerrey's staff — Pete included — had worked what amounted to a second full-time job on Nelson's campaign. When Nelson won, Pete was job-hunting.

That is when Daub made the offer. Daub hired Pete knowing exactly what it looked like: a Republican mayor bringing someone straight out of a Democratic senator's office. Pete served as Daub's point person on the streetcar / light rail project.

The same Kerrey network then organized the mayoral campaign that ousted Daub. When Fahey took office, many of those former colleagues — long-time friends — saw Pete as a turncoat and made him pay for it. The natural next move — transitioning into Fahey's administration as the riverfront guy — was closed off.

Pete's response was to found **BTTR** as a nonprofit, channeling the riverfront work into civic life instead. That pivot led eventually to Ameristar, and from there to a career in Workforce Management.

Kim Castellano had been offered the position of elementary school principal at Oak Harbor Public Schools — the largest district on Whidbey Island, WA, near the naval air station, north of Seattle across Puget Sound. She declined to stay in Omaha because Pete was working for Mayor Daub. Daub remembered this unprompted, thirty years later.

**Note for editors:** This exchange — unscripted, at the end of the interview in Daub's own home — puts Pete inside the history of the riverfront story. The political cost was real and lasting for both men. Daub saying *"Boy, did I get shit for hiring you"* is the most candid line in the full interview. And the reason BTTR exists as a nonprofit is directly traceable to this moment.
