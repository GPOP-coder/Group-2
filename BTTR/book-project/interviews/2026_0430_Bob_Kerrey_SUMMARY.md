# Bob Kerrey Interview Summary
## April 30, 2026

**Interviewees:** Senator Bob Kerrey  
**Interviewers:** John Dechant, James Fogarty, Pete Castellano  
**Duration:** ~52 minutes (transcript 19 pages)  
**Format:** Recorded interview at/near Bob Kerrey Bridge

## Key Themes

1. **Rivers as Essential Natural Resources**
   - Missouri River as Nebraska's most important feature; entire state drains into it
   - Rivers as unifying force connecting rural and urban Nebraska
   - Need to balance development with ecological respect for natural boundaries

2. **The Bridge as Practical Solution (Not Grand Vision)**
   - Originated from observation: "I just saw people using the bike trails in Nebraska and there was some limitations"
   - "I have approximately one good idea every 20 years. This is one of them."
   - Success came from recognizing Iowa's advantage (public land via constitution) and solving the connector problem

3. **Multi-Agency Collaboration & Built-in Tension**
   - ~12-13 federal agencies involved; each reports to different subcommittee/authority
   - Agencies came together because: (a) it's a good project, (b) they knew they'd "get in trouble if they don't"
   - Built-in incentive problem: agencies rewarded for performance within their silo, not for cross-agency success
   - Pre-2001 political alignment helped; expressed concern Trump would have killed it if timeline shifted to 2016

4. **Leadership Through Delegation**
   - "All I had to do is persuade Dick Shelby to give us $17 million and after that I was useless"
   - Trusted Pete Castellano and team to do the detailed work; Bob attended meetings but let competent people execute
   - Philosophy: "If you got really smart, competent people like yourself...you just let them do it"

5. **Staying Young at Heart & Unlikely Partnerships**
   - Emphasized importance of listening critically and finding opportunities for collaboration across divides
   - Warned against cynicism ("it's a disease") — tendency of older people to cross from skepticism to disbelief in capacity for good
   - Referenced Seamus Heaney's *The Cure at Troy*: "People get hurt, they get hard, no poem, verse or song can overcome a wrong inflicted and endured"

6. **Bridge as Metaphor**
   - "We're constantly going over bridges" — bridge between moments, time as crossing
   - Cormac McCarthy's *The Crossing* trilogy referenced for exploration of change at border crossings
   - "We're all either building a bridge to somebody where you're trying to develop a relationship with them or you're crossing a bridge"

## Notable Quotes (with timestamps)

**On the idea's origin (35:13):**
> "No, I just saw people using the bike trails in Nebraska and there was some limitations on that. And then somebody pointed out to me that the reason Iowa has more is that they've used the public land. That have become public land. And then this brilliant insight, let's build a pedestrian bridge."

**On success metrics (24:17):**
> "It was a good project and lots of people were involved and lots of people were using it. If we'd have built the bridge and nobody used it, nobody went on it, but there are people walking and riding bikes across that 365 days a year. And on beautiful days, they're jamming up. It's become a park all by itself."

**On delegation (42:18):**
> "There's no need to get people that can actually do the micro stuff to get in there and try to do micro stuff and act like you're a big guy...If you got really smart, competent people like yourself and people like that, that are involved with it, you just let them do it."

**On wealth & character (27:05-27:11):**
> "Wealth just allows you to be a little more what you already were. So if you start off a jerk and you become wealthy, you can become a really big jerk and sue all your friends and make their lives miserable. But otherwise, if you got good character, you can use that wealth to do good."

**On rivers as unification (22:38):**
> "It doesn't take much when you look, you get the Missouri River. One of the most important tributaries in the world coming right by our city, right through our state, the entire state drains into that river. This river connects to the entire state of Nebraska. It's a constant question, how do we unite the rural and the urban world? Follow the damn rivers. The rivers are uniting us, even though we may have political problems getting it done."
